5. `\sqrt` WAS NEVER MODELLED AT ALL.
   `to_tex` refused any span containing a radical glyph outright -- one line,
   `# see LIMITS`. Cost: 828 refusals across the 102 documents. The author
   wrote 70 roots in 41 equations, 9.7% of the corpus, and not one could ever
   be read. MathPix reads 68 of the 70.

   The machinery was already there. A radical is `_merge_overlines`' own shape
   -- a bar with glyphs below and nothing above -- plus a sign immediately to
   the bar's LEFT. TeX starts the vinculum exactly at the sign's right edge
   (161.6 to 161.6, measured on wzlxjtu-020), so the pairing is not a guess.
   The blanket guard MOVED below the merge rather than away: a radical that
   found no vinculum still defers exactly as before.

6. BIG DELIMITERS AND RADICAL SIGNS ARE THE SAME FURNITURE AS A BIG OPERATOR.
   wzlxjtu-020 sets `\delta(\sqrt{\gamma}L_{ren})` as two bands:

       base 480.8   parenleftbig@146  radical@152  parenrightbig@189
       base 470.9   delta@139  gamma@162  L@168  r@177  e@180  n@184

   Everything tall on one band, everything it encloses on the other. 731's
   operator rejoin only recognised `tex.kind == "bigop"`, so a band of pure
   furniture never rejoined the row it was furniture FOR. `_attach_scripts`
   already groups "bigop" and "delimiter" together for this exact reason.

7. A RADICAL SIGN NOW FOLLOWS ITS VINCULUM.
   Being raised, the sign can be grouped with the row ABOVE the one it belongs
   to. That left an unpaired radical on a row that had otherwise assembled
   correctly -- and an unpaired radical defers the whole span, so a good row
   was thrown away for a glyph that was never its own.
