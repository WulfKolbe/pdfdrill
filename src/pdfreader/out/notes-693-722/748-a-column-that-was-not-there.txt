It is found from the distribution of row left edges, and a row that already
spans both columns also contributes "the x just after its widest internal
gap". On a page of display equations that clause reads their internal
whitespace as column evidence. The acceptance test was

    n >= 0.1 * ordered[0][1]

-- a tenth of the FIRST CANDIDATE's own count, which is itself usually 1, so
a single piece of evidence always passed. The page's real mode had SEVEN
rows at x=84; the accepted "column" had ONE.

that x is 3.4pt, eq 4's is 5.0pt. The wider one crossed the split.

Now a second column has to be named by at least two rows. One row's internal
gap is not a column.
