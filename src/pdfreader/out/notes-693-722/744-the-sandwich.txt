744 — wzlxjtu-009 EQUATION 3: THE "3" BELONGED TO THE LINE BELOW

You called it subtle. It is the worst kind of error this reader makes,
because it is symmetric and silent: one row GAINS something and the next
row LOSES it, and both still look like mathematics.

    gold      M_0 = 2m e^{-3\sigma} \cos \phi^3 \sinh \phi^0
              M_3 = -2i g e^{\sigma} \sin \phi^3

    pdf2mmd   M_0 = 2m e^{-3\sigma} \cos \phi_{\sigma}^{3} \sinh \phi_{3}^{0}
              M_3 = -2i g e \sin \phi

Two invented subscripts on one row; two superscripts missing from the next.
Nothing marks either.


THE GEOMETRY SAYS ONE THING AND THE GROUPER DID THE OTHER
---------------------------------------------------------
    row M_0        baseline 170.16
    the two strays baseline 157.66
    row M_3        baseline 152.72

The strays are 4.94pt above the row below and 12.5pt below the row above --
they are NEARER to where they belong, and went to the other one anyway. They
are the superscripts of `e^{\sigma}` and `\phi^3` on the M_3 row, and a
superscript sits exactly 4.93pt above its base at this size, which is what
every other script on the page does too.

There is already a pass for this, and it could not fire: it rescues a band
that is ENTIRELY scripts, and skips any group holding full-size glyphs. A
script absorbed into a full-size row is precisely the case it cannot reach,
and precisely the case that yields a wrong reading rather than a missing
one.


THE STREAM SETTLES IT
---------------------
    stream 1698  'e'       row M_3
    stream 1699  '\sigma'  row M_0      <- wrong
    stream 1700  '\sin'    row M_3
    stream 1703  '\phi'    row M_3
    stream 1704  '3'       row M_0      <- wrong
    stream 1708  '('       row M_3

Each stray sits BETWEEN two glyphs of the row it belongs to. A glyph
sandwiched in the stream belongs to its neighbours -- both of them must
agree, because one would be mere adjacency -- and it must be script-size, so
this moves scripts and never prose.

AND A STREAM NEIGHBOUR IS NOT ALWAYS A NEIGHBOUR ON THE PAGE. The first
attempt fixed `\sigma` and left `3` exactly as it was: the glyph following
`\phi` in the stream is 1705, the `(` of an EARLIER EQUATION'S NUMBER, 163pt
up the page, because TeX emits a tag after the body it belongs to. The scan
now steps past any neighbour more than two ems off this glyph's band -- a
neighbour on another band says nothing about this glyph.

Both rows now come out character-for-character gold.
