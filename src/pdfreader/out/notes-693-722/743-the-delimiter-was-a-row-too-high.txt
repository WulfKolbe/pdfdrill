743 — THE 75 EMPTY DELIMITER PAIRS

    \frac{\theta_p(pq^2t^2A^{-2}B^2)\theta_p\bigl((pq)^{-1}q^{-1}t^{-4}A^2B^{-2}\bigr)}
         {\bigl( \bigr) \bigl( \bigr) \bigl( \bigr) \bigl( \bigr) \bigl( \bigr)}

Seventy-five of those over the corpus, in 14 documents, concentrated in
090-095. Brackets with the mathematics gone from between them -- wrong
output that looks like right output, which is the one thing this reader is
not allowed to produce.


WHERE THEY COME FROM
--------------------
wzlxjtu-093's first line is the clean case. The line runs y 666.4..699.7
with its fraction bar at 672.2, and its glyphs fall on five baselines:

    base 687.7  ABOVE   (cid:0)(cid:1)                    <- numerator's own \big( )
    base 682.4  ABOVE   22-22-1-1-42-2
    base 678.0  ABOVE   theta(pqtAB)theta(pq)qtAB
    base 676.2  ABOVE   pp
    base 670.0  below   ( ) ( ) ( ) ( ) ( ) ( )           <- TEN DELIMITERS, NOTHING ELSE

Everything below the bar is delimiters and nothing else. What they enclose
-- `t^{-2}`, at baseline 660.3 -- is in the NEXT BAND.

A `\bigl(` is set on the MATHS AXIS, not on the baseline of what it
encloses, so it sits a fixed distance above its own contents and baseline
clustering files it one row too high. Where the contents happened to land in
the same merged band it worked; where the band boundary fell between them
the delimiters were orphaned, and a pair with nothing between it prints as
`\bigl( \bigr)`.

The grouper already knew about this for BIG OPERATORS -- `key()` lowers a
`bigop` by 0.7 em for exactly this reason. Delimiters were not in it.


THE CONSTANT, MEASURED
----------------------
Every big delimiter in the first 50 documents, against the nearest full-size
glyph beside it, as a fraction of its own size:

    big    n=67   0.81 em   (39 of 67 exactly 0.81)
    Big    n=12   1.11 em   ( 9 of 12 exactly 1.11)
    bigg   n=37   1.41 em
    Bigg   n=2    1.71 em

0.81, 1.11, 1.41, 1.71 -- a step of 0.30 em per size. That is not four
constants, it is how TeX grows \big \Big \bigg \Bigg, and the table says so.

(The medians for `bigg` are noisy because "nearest full-size glyph to the
right" sometimes picks the wrong reference. The MODE is the signal, and it
is exact.)

THE SEVEN THAT REMAIN ARE A DIFFERENT CLASS
-------------------------------------------
They are `\left( \right)` and `\left. \right)`, not `\bigl( \bigr)`: six in
wzlxjtu-074 and one in 011. Those come from EXTENSIBLE fences -- a delimiter
built from stacked pieces around a matrix -- where each matrix row is its
own line and the fence has no single baseline to correct. That is the matrix
case, and it belongs with the multi-baseline row problem of 727 rather than
here.
