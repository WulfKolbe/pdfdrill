694 — what a LaTeX space leaves in the PDF stream, measured

THE EXPERIMENT
--------------
A table of all thirteen spacing forms, `$a<space>b$` per row, compiled with
pdflatex, then every glyph on the page read with its advance and position.

RESULT — NOT ONE OF THEM EMITS A GLYPH
--------------------------------------
Every form is a pure ADVANCE of the current point. Zero glyphs between the
`a` and the `b` in all thirteen rows. The gaps, in em:

    \!           -0.167          \enspace      +0.500
    (none)        0.000          \quad         +1.000
    \,           +0.167          \qquad        +2.000
    \:           +0.222          \             +0.333
    \;           +0.278          ~             +0.333
    \hspace{10pt} +0.913         text space    +0.333
    \mspace{6mu} +0.333

These are TeX's own numbers, not a fit: 3mu = 1/6 em, 4mu = 2/9, 5mu = 5/18.
`\!` is NEGATIVE. `\ `, `~` and an ordinary text space are identical at
+1/3 em and cannot be told apart by width.

WHAT IT MEANS FOR A GLYPH-BASED READER
--------------------------------------
Spacing is invisible to anything that reads glyphs. It exists only as
geometry, and it must be inferred or it is lost. pdf2mmd joined maths with a
single blank, so all thirteen collapsed to `$a b$` -- including `$ab$`, which
has no space at all, and `\!`, which has a negative one.

RECOVERING THE COMMAND — TWO MEASUREMENTS SAID NO
-------------------------------------------------
Mapping every band back to its command looked obvious and was wrong twice:

1. TeX inserts spacing AUTOMATICALLY by atom class -- 4mu round a binary
   operator, 5mu round a relation. Those are exactly `\:` and `\;` by width.
   Emitting them explicitly measured 48.8 spacing commands per 1000 tokens on
   a real paper, far more than any author writes, and a recompile would add
   TeX's spacing again on top.

2. Narrowed to `\quad`/`\qquad` only, it then fired on ALIGN environments:
   `\vartheta^{\alpha}\quad= E_{i}^{\alpha}`, where the gap before the
   relation is the alignment's, not the author's.

WHAT SHIPPED
------------
Only what geometry alone can settle:

    gap < -0.08 em   ->  \!        (nothing automatic produces a negative gap)
    |gap| < 0.08     ->  ""        (`$ab$` stays `$ab$`)
    otherwise        ->  " "

Corpus after the change: 14 documents, 387 pages, every projection rate
unchanged from 693. Three corpus tests had encoded the old fixed join and
now assert what the measured geometry implies.

NOT SETTLED
-----------
Separating `\,` from a binary operator's automatic 4mu needs the ATOM
CLASSES (ord/bin/rel/op/open/close/punct), which this projector does not
model. That is the piece of information the geometry cannot supply, and it
is the same shape as the row/column threshold problem: the rule is in TeX's
tables, not in the page.
