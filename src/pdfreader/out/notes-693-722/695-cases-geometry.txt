695 — the `cases` construct: measured geometry, and the prerequisite

WHAT IT LOOKS LIKE IN THE MODEL
-------------------------------
From the-semantics-of-metaprogramming-in-prolog, page 3. The author wrote a
`cases`; four separate display blocks came out:

    $$\Biggl\{$$        $$x \in N at =$$
    $$\text{if} x = 0$$ $$\text{if} x^{\prime} \in N at ...$$

The rows, top to bottom:

    y=356.6  x=[203.5,211.5]  the brace, alone on its row
    y=348.4  x=[213.0,251.1]  `if x = 0`                 <- case 1
    y=339.6  x=[157.7,201.6]  `x in Nat =`               <- the LHS
    y=331.2  x=[213.0,341.0]  `if x' in Nat and ...`     <- case 2

The arrangement is unambiguous and it repeats on the same page for a second
construct (`x in R_a^G =` at y=273.8):

    the FENCE is a single CMEX glyph, `braceleftBigg`
    the LHS is entirely LEFT of the fence and vertically CENTRED on it
    the cases all start at one x, RIGHT of the fence, within its span

So the reconstruction rule needs no new statistics:

    LHS \begin{cases} case1 \\ case2 \end{cases}

THE PREREQUISITE, AND IT IS A DEFECT OF OURS
--------------------------------------------
    braceleftBigg  CMEX10  rect=[203.5, 346.7, 211.5, 356.6]  height 10.0pt

Ten points. The brace visibly spans from the first case to the last, roughly
25pt. The height is wrong because `_sane_rect` REPAIRED it: CMEX10 declares a
-2.359em descent that misplaces every big operator, and the repair replaces
the declared box with a plausible one around the baseline.

That repair is what made big operators and radicals work. It also destroys
the one measurement a fence reconstruction depends on -- how far the fence
reaches. Both readings are needed:

    the repaired box   for grouping the fence onto a row
    the declared box   for knowing what it spans

The fix is to keep the original bbox on the glyph beside the repaired one,
not to choose between them. Nothing can be built on top until that exists.

NOT ATTEMPTED
-------------
The reconstruction itself. The geometry above is from ONE document and two
instances on one page; a `cases` with three or more branches, a `matrix`, an
`array` with alignment, and a fence built from STACKED pieces
(`braceleftBig` + `braceex` + `braceleftbt`) rather than one glyph are all
unmeasured, and the stacked form is the common one in older TeX.
