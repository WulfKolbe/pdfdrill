THE CORRECTION TO HIS WORDING, WHICH MAKES IT SHARPER
-----------------------------------------------------
"2-line expression" is what he SEES; it is not `\\` in the source.
wzlxjtu-006 gold #1 is one LaTeX line:

    L = \prod_{i=0}^3 e^{\phi^i K^i}

It came back as THREE blocks:   `L =`  /  `\prod_{\phi^i K^i} i=0`  /  `e`

and gold #3, also one line:

    m^2 l^2 = \Delta(\Delta-5)      ->   m^{2} l^{2} = \Delta(\Delta - 5)

perfect, superscripts and all. The difference between them is not line
count. It is whether the row puts an EXPRESSION on a baseline other than
the main one. `m^2` is a script on the main baseline. `\prod`'s limits,
and a fraction's numerator, are sub-expressions each on a baseline of
their own.

That is 727's result stated from the other end. 727 said `_attach_scripts`
models one baseline with scripts hung around it, and refuses on the
"same baseline but excluded" branch when asked to describe several.
730 says the same thing measured over the whole corpus: the reader's
competence boundary IS `_attach_scripts`'s model boundary, exactly.

    scripts on one baseline   ->  works   (13.6%, and the misses there are
                                           ordinary reading errors)
    sub-expression on its own ->  0.7%    (shattered into one block per
    baseline                               band it could not attach)

WHY 817 > 421 IS NOT "A BIT OVER-SPLIT"
---------------------------------------
Because the 817 are not equations. They are the fragments left when a
display row is cut at every baseline the attach pass could not bind.
A count of them measures how badly the row fell apart, nothing else --
which is why "more than MathPix" means 100% wrong and not "close".

WHAT THIS DOES NOT SAY
----------------------
It does not name the fix. 727 already said what is needed (a pass that
can describe a row of several baselines each carrying a sub-expression)
and that it is not a tweak to `_attach_scripts`. 730 adds only the size
of the prize: 281 of 421 equations, 67% of the corpus, all of it now at
0.7%.

It also does not excuse the flat 13.6%. Single-baseline rows still lose
6 in 7 to ordinary reading errors. But that is a different defect and a
much smaller one, and it should not be worked on first.
