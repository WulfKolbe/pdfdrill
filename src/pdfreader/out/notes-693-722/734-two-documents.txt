734/735 — wzlxjtu-043 AND wzlxjtu-045, BOTH LOOKED AT

Two reports, two different defects, and one measurement error found on the
way that mattered more than either.


043 — THE EQUATIONS WERE NOT LOST. THEY WERE CUT IN HALF.
---------------------------------------------------------
Equations 2 and 3 were read CORRECTLY and then emitted as inline `$...$` in
the middle of the prose, while their own continuation rows stood beside them
as displays. Nothing was missing; the head of each equation — the part
carrying `\delta_1^c(0) =`, which is what NAMES it — had simply stopped
being part of the equation.

`is_display` judges one line at a time and its test is INDENT. Measured on
that page, column margin 397px, 12pt type:

    eq 1   row 1 x=427 (indent 30)   row 2 x=489 (92)    both display   right
    eq 2   row 1 x=409 (indent 12)   row 2 x=507 (110)   row 1 INLINE   wrong
    eq 3   row 1 x=409 (indent 12)   row 2 x=507 (110)   row 1 INLINE   wrong

A multi-row display is not obliged to indent every row equally. This author
pulls the continuation LEFT — the gold source says `\hspace{-0.15cm}` — so
the first row barely clears the margin and the second clears it easily.

THE FIX IS NOT A THRESHOLD. A row beginning `-`, `+` or `=` CANNOT BEGIN AN
EQUATION: a binary operator needs a left operand, and the only place that
operand can be is the line above. So a recognised display row that starts
with one claims the line above it, provided that line is maths, is not
prose, and stands directly over it. Walked upward, so a three-row display
claims both.

043 equations 2 and 3 now come out character-for-character gold.


A MEASUREMENT ERROR, AND IT WAS LARGE
-------------------------------------
Checking that, the two readings differed on NOTHING but `\mid` against `|`.
They are the same character; `\mid` only adds relation spacing. Gold writes
`|` for "given that", this reader writes `\mid`, and the dialect table that
exists precisely to neutralise such differences did not have it.

    with `\mid` counted as dialect:   85 correct -> 136

FIFTY-ONE EQUATIONS WERE ALREADY CORRECT AND WERE BEING SCORED WRONG. That
is not a gain from any fix in this unit; it is a number that was wrong
before. Both sides were re-measured with the corrected table so the
comparison stays honest, MathPix included.


045 — THE FRACTION BARS WERE THROWN AWAY
----------------------------------------
Different defect. The display is ONE two-row equation and it came apart into
NINE pieces, with two spans deferred `assembly`.

The cause was upstream of all of it. The PDF draws six rules on that page;
the reader kept four. The two it dropped were the fraction bars, and they
were dropped by this guard:

    if (x1 - x0) > 12.0 * size: return "separator"

which exists to stop a code listing's border being read as a fraction. But
TeX sets the fraction rule AS WIDE AS THE WIDER OF NUMERATOR AND
DENOMINATOR, so a long denominator makes a long bar:

    \frac{1}{\sum_{i=1}^{n} I\{D_i=1, M_i=0, T_i=1\}}

160.2pt in 12pt type — 13.35 em, against a 12 em test. It failed by 16pt.
And with no bar there is no fraction, so the numerator, the denominator and
the head of the equation stayed three separate bands.

A listing border is told from a fraction bar by the same rule TeX drew it
with: ONE OF THE TWO GROUPS REACHES BOTH ENDS OF THE BAR. Code inside a
frame is inset from both margins and reaches neither.

045 is now PARTLY fixed and I am not going to call it more than that. Both
bars are classified `fraction`, and the numerator, denominator and head are
in one line where they belong. The span then still refuses, `reason=fraction`
— the assembly cannot yet describe a row carrying several baselines each
with its own sub-expression. That is the open item named in 727 and it is
not a tweak.
