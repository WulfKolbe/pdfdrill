THE LOOK-AHEAD HE ASKED FOR
---------------------------
Nothing INSIDE a display says where it stops. The producer pops the graphics
state and the current point with it. The line AFTER it announces itself --
by starting at the BODY MARGIN rather than at the display indent.

724's `line_starts` returns only the y. That bounds a LINE and not an
EXPRESSION; the x is the whole signal and I discarded it.

    psstream.line_origins(shows)   -> [(x, y), ...] per line
    psstream.display_runs(origins) -> [("body"|"display", [line indices])]

The body margin is the x most lines begin at. A display ends at the first
following line that returns to it.

NOT WIRED IN
------------
This is the boundary, not the change. Two reasons to stop here.

First, 725 measured what happens when a better row structure meets
`_attach_scripts`: blocks 817 -> 98, projection 86.4% -> 62.4%. The pass
cannot describe a row carrying several baselines each with its own
sub-expression, and 727 measured that binding by stream does not fix that
either. Nothing has changed about that.

Second, the honest metric is now 5% of equations delivered whole (729), not
a block count. Any wiring of this should be judged by that number, and I
would rather measure it against a working implementation than predict it.
