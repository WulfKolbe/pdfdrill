The `\biggl[` is EMITTED BEFORE the `m e` and PLACED AFTER them. TeX has to

Band p1l7 is a LEFTOVER: every exponent of the row, plus 400 and 401, which
are the numerator and denominator of `\frac{1}{8}` -- same x, full size,
one above the row and one below it.

There is a pass that returns a displaced band's glyphs to their row using
exactly this stream evidence. IT SKIPS ANY BAND HOLDING A FULL-SIZE GLYPH,
because such a band is a row rather than a band of scripts. The two digits
of `\frac{1}{8}` are full size, so p1l7 is disqualified, and every exponent
on the row stays behind with them.

That is the whole defect: ONE FRACTION IN A SUPERSCRIPT BAND DISQUALIFIES
THE BAND, and the row loses all its exponents.

WHAT THE REAL FIX LOOKS LIKE
----------------------------
Not a tie-break. The two bands are not symmetric in the evidence -- I was
using the wrong evidence. p1l7's glyphs are at 527.39, 530.54 and 514.26
around a row at 522.46: they are a SUPERSCRIPT LINE, a FRACTION NUMERATOR
and a FRACTION DENOMINATOR of that row, and each of those is a known
relationship with its own geometry. Deciding "which band is the row" by
counting glyphs throws all of that away.

The band that owns a baseline is the one whose glyphs sit ON it. That is
the direction to take next, and it needs the fraction pass and the script
pass to agree about one row rather than each guessing separately -- which
is the 727 problem again, from a new side.
