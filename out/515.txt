--------------------------------

scale-stable all count as the ink AGREEING with the reading, so an N row is
never flagged. johnston carries 763 N rows and not one of them is in the

357 of 543 (66%) sit at confidence >= 0.9. That band is already measured:
out/465 sampled twenty rows drawn from it corpus-wide and found 18 purely
typographic differences, 1 typographic plus a crop overrun, 1 borderline and
ZERO unambiguous content errors. `ink flag AND conf >= 0.9` is a
rendering-difference detector, not a defect detector. Two thirds of the
section is therefore the class least likely to repay reading.

WHAT ORDERS THE ROWS: THE COMPONENT DELTA
------------------------------------------
Every ink code carries a signed component delta (`C|+817`, `W|-1`). Over the
543:

    median 2, 90th percentile 10, max 817

    |delta| >= 20                    37 rows
    |delta| >= 50                    17 rows

37 is the actionable number the task was reaching for — and the cut is drawn
on magnitude, not on class.

ONE THING NOT TO MISREAD AS A FINDING
--------------------------------------
Every row with |delta| >= 20 is component, and it would be easy to report
that as two measures agreeing. It is not. `inkconvert.flag_of` DEFINES
component as `comp_delta > NOISE_COMP_DELTA` (= 2). The band is a sub-range
of C by construction. It says the delta orders the rows usefully; it does not
say two independent signals concur.

DOES THE BAND HOLD OFF johnston?
---------------------------------
Across every library document carrying both an ink file and a projection
(1,101 documents, 4,998 flagged rows):

    flagged                        4,998
    component (C)                  1,749
    weak      (W)                  3,249
    |delta| >= 20                    320   (6.4%)
    |delta| >= 50                    167   (3.3%)

The band is a tail everywhere, not a johnston artefact. It also degrades
sanely: most documents flag under 30 rows and show none, which is correct —
they had no tail to show.
