  ! Missing character: There is no g ("67) in font rsfs10!

`mathrsfs` is uppercase-only. MathPix's `\mathscr{g}` renders as nothing at
all, so the row was an empty cell, not a mis-set one. That changes the

  \mathscr{g}   blank            the state before out/229

The refinement was not a regression. It was a partial fix recorded as a whole
one, and the half that was wrong is the half nothing measured.

A FIRST MEASUREMENT THAT WAS NOT GOOD ENOUGH, AND WHY IT IS RECORDED
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Before the overlap test I built a five-tuple-style comparison — components,
holes, aspect, ink fraction — and it said `\mathcal{J}` 12.42 against
`\mathscr{J}` 13.93, i.e. the opposite of the eye and of the final answer.

It was wrong because the instrument was: the printed glyph measured 4
components and 5 holes for a single connected letterform, which is a
threshold fragmenting a 50-px glyph upscaled 8x, not a property of the ink.
And the distance function was one I invented, weighting aspect and ink
fraction by numbers I chose.

The fix was to stop deriving a statistic and compare the pixels: same
normalisation, direct intersection over union. That is the measurement in the
table above. The first numbers are here because a reader who sees 12.42 vs
13.93 somewhere else should know it was rejected and on what grounds.

THE METHODOLOGICAL POINT WORTH KEEPING
--------------------------------------
Three sizes looked flat; twelve found four departures between the sampled
points. The same shape as sampling the first 24 lexicographic permutations
and calling it a sweep — and the same shape as the first measurement in this
report, which sampled four derived statistics and missed what the pixels
show. A sample that is not dense enough to see the thing it is looking for
returns "flat" and "no difference" with equal confidence either way.
