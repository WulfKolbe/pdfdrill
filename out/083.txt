083 - MathPix's verbatim: its own rendering, or the author's environment?
==============================================================================

THE SPLIT
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   tex.zip documents containing \begin{verbatim}      67

     ATTRIBUTABLE - an author e-print exists to compare   10
       author also opens verbatim  -> author's           2
       author does NOT             -> MathPix's own      8

     NOT ATTRIBUTABLE - no e-print at all                 57

READ THE THIRD ROW FIRST
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   57 of the 67 cannot be attributed at all. Without an author source
   there is nothing to compare the MathPix output against, so no method
   reaches them — they are neither 'MathPix's own' nor 'the author's', they
   are unknown, and counting them as either would be inventing the answer.

   The split is therefore measured on 10 documents, 15% of the population
   the question asks about. That is the honest denominator.

WHAT THE ATTRIBUTABLE 10 SHOW
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   8 of 10 are MathPix's own — it emitted verbatim where the author
   opened no verbatim environment. What those authors had instead:

     algorithm                4
     none of the four         3
     lstlisting               1
     verbatim                 1
     algorithm, verbatim      1

   The dominant case is algorithm. Combined with out/078's finding that
   MathPix never emits the algorithm environment — 0 of 235 tex.zip and 0
   of 3,297 lines.json against 97 authors who use it — this says MathPix
   renders a pseudocode float as EITHER lstlisting or verbatim. out/078
   measured that branch directly: of 15 author-algorithm documents with
   both sources, 11 became lstlisting and 4 became verbatim.

   So MathPix's verbatim is largely NOT a code environment at all. It is
   its fallback container for a structured float it cannot reproduce.

WHY THIS MATTERS FOR THE INGEST
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   A CodeListing built from MathPix verbatim would frequently contain
   pseudocode, not source. On the attributable evidence that is the
   majority case, not an edge: 5 of the 10 attributable documents have an
   author algorithm environment behind MathPix's verbatim.

   This is the same reason out/079 refuses to parse algorithm into
   CodeListing from the author side. The rule holds on both sides of the
   pipeline: pseudocode is a different object, and a container that happens
   to hold monospaced text is not evidence of what is in it.

LIMIT OF THE METHOD
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   'Author also opens verbatim' is a document-level test, not a
   region-level one. A document where the author uses verbatim ONCE and
   MathPix emits it five times counts as 'author's' here, and 4 of those 5
   may still be MathPix's own. Attributing occurrence by occurrence needs
   position matching between the two sources, which this does not do.

   The direction of that error is knowable though: it can only
   UNDERSTATE how much of the verbatim is MathPix's own. The 8-of-10
   figure is a floor.
