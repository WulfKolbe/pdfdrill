What the residual column measures

Each report pairs MathPix's reading of an equation with the scan it was read from. inkdrill typesets that reading, renders it, and compares the render against the scan crop by connected components — the separate marks of ink each image contains — and reports the difference between them.

A code such as C|+4 reads: component class, four more marks in the scan than in the render. The letter is the class of difference; the signed number is how many marks the scan has that the render does not (or, when negative, the other way round).

Kclean — the two images carry the same marks
Nnoise — inside the measured noise band
Wweak — a difference that does not survive rescaling
Sstable — a difference that does survive rescaling
Ccomponent — the mark counts differ beyond the noise band

A flag is not an error

A flag on an equation MathPix is confident about is usually a rendering difference, not a reading error. Twenty flagged rows at high confidence were examined by eye one at a time: 18 of 20 were purely typographic, one was typographic plus a region overrun, and one was borderline. Not one of the twenty carried a symbol MathPix had got wrong.

What the twenty actually disagreed about was how the mathematics is set, not what it says: auto-sized delimiters against the page's fixed-size parentheses (13 rows), trailing punctuation that lives in a separate field and is omitted from the render by design (8), \bar against a wider \overline (3), \frac against \dfrac (2), an upright Φ set italic on the page (2), and limits beside the integral sign rather than above it (1).

So the residual column is best read as “these two images differ, and by how much” — a place to look, not a verdict. It is most useful where the confidence column is also low: a doubted reading that additionally disagrees with its own scan is worth a human eye. A confident reading with a residual flag is, on the evidence above, far more likely to be a typesetting difference than a mistake.

Where a document shows no residual column, the comparison was not available for it — either no measurement has been run, or the report's own table could not be read reliably enough to pair measurements with equations. The confidence column is unaffected and still applies.
