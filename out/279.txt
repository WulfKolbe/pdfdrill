279 — what the tex.zip contains, and why matching is neither vision nor arithmetic
==============================================================================

WHAT IS IN THE ZIPS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
    tex.zip files                       1,216   (0 unreadable)
      with image files                    931
      with NO image files                 285
    image files                        20,582
    \includegraphics                   20,670
    float environments                 16,537

285 zips carry no image at all — 1510.06699's, for instance, holds exactly one
`.tex` and nothing else. So "the tex.zip has the pictures" is true of 77% of
documents, not all of them.

DO THE FILENAMES CARRY A PAGE NUMBER? YES — AND FOUR MORE NUMBERS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
    347c6137-8dc3-4f41-86ab-77de4ae1347f-04_548_852_666_640.jpg
    └── process id ──────────────────────┘ └pg┘ └─h─┘└─w─┘└y ┘└x ┘

    filenames carrying a digit group   20,565 of 20,582
    \includegraphics targets likewise  20,645 of 20,670

The `.tex` references each image by that filename with the extension stripped —
`\includegraphics[alt={},max width=\textwidth]{…-04_548_852_666_640}` — so the
reference carries the page too.

The four numbers after the page are not an index. They are the REGION:
`height_width_top_left_y_top_left_x`, the same four keys 278 found on every
diagram, chart and table line.

VERIFIED, NOT INFERRED FROM THE SHAPE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Every parseable filename was looked up as a 5-tuple `(page, height, width,
top_left_y, top_left_x)` against the regions in that document's own lines.json:

    documents with a canonical lines.json + tex.zip    1,210
    image filenames parsed                           20,287
    EXACT match to a region in lines.json             20,276   (99.95%)
    no match                                             11   (0.05%)
    filenames that do not parse                          17

    what the matched regions are:
      diagram 17,787 · chart 2,099 · code 193 · text 126
      simple_cell 23 · complex_cell 15

So matching a tex.zip image to a docmodel object is not vision, and it is not
arithmetic either. It is a dictionary lookup on a key both sides already hold.
The task's premise was conservative: page alone would have needed arithmetic to
disambiguate several figures on one page; the filename disambiguates them
itself.

THE 11 THAT DO NOT MATCH
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
    2505.0046v1   5    lines.json carries NO image_id at all, and its tex.zip
                       is dated 2026-04-04 against a lines.json of 2026-08-03 —
                       four months apart, so they are two different MathPix runs
                       and the regions have no reason to agree.
    2510.11170v2  4
    BH1           2

11 in 20,287 is 0.05%, and the largest cause is a stale pairing rather than a
disagreement about geometry.

FLOAT POSITION CARRIES NOTHING
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
    figure[h]   12,795      table[h]   3,734
    table[]         5       figure[]       2      figure[!t]   1

16,529 of 16,537 float environments are `[h]` — "here". That is MathPix
emitting a fixed specifier, not a statement about where the float sat on the
page. Anything read out of the float position would be reading a constant. The
page is in the filename, which is where it should be looked for.

A MEASUREMENT ERROR OF MINE, CORRECTED MID-TASK
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The first corpus pass reported 17 unmatched across the same three documents.
It picked each folder's lines.json with `glob("*.lines.json")[0]`, and three of
these folders hold more than one:

    2510.11170v2.glossaries.lines.json · 2510.11170v2.lines.json · test.lines.json
    BH1-9add65ca.lines.json · BH1.lines.json

So the tex.zip was being compared against a glossaries extract and a variant
run. Comparing against the canonical `<stem>.lines.json` gives 11, not 17. I
caught it because a second script written to characterise the mismatches found
none at all — two of my own scans disagreeing, which is a reason to stop and
not to pick the more convenient answer.

Nothing changed in this task.
