SYMBOL-CONSISTENCY — 55 CASES, AND ONLY 24 ARE SUSPICIOUS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
`gaps` does not answer this. Its `symbol_undefined` rule asks whether a Greek
letter is DECLARED anywhere; it says nothing about the same letter being
written three ways in one book. I computed that separately, over each
document's own equations, grouping every Greek symbol by its accent and font
wrapper (\bar \hat \tilde \vec \dot \boldsymbol \mathbf \mathcal … versus
plain). A symbol in two or more forms is a consistency case.

   55 symbols appear in more than one form.

But most are DELIBERATE NOTATION, not inconsistency, and reporting all 55 as
findings would be wrong. In Geometrodynamics:

   \psi     plain×219, bar×102, hat×16, widehat×8

\bar{\psi} is the Dirac adjoint. It is a different object from \psi and is
supposed to look different. Likewise \boldsymbol{\epsilon} × 42 against
\epsilon × 46 in Lyche is vector against scalar.

So I split them on frequency: a non-dominant form seen at most twice against a
dominant form seen at least fifty times is too rare to be a convention.

   deliberate notation (balanced forms)     31
   SINGLETON decorations                    24   <- the actionable ones

   2103.01507      \lambda  plain×313 vs hat×1
                   \pi      plain×142 vs hat×1
                   \sigma   plain×251 vs tilde×1
   Geometrodynamics \Gamma  plain×142 vs tilde×1, widehat×1
                   \Psi     plain×207 vs dot×2, widetilde×2, hat×1
                   \Omega   plain×336 vs mathcal×1
   Numerical LA    \alpha   plain×116 vs bar×1
                   \rho     plain×89  vs bar×1

One \hat{\lambda} among 313 plain λ is either a genuine hat the book uses once,
or an OCR artefact. That is a question worth a human eye; 313 plain λ and 102
\bar\psi are not.

THE 112 UNDECLARED SYMBOLS MEAN SOMETHING NARROWER THAN THEY SOUND
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Every one of the 55 multi-form symbols is also in the undeclared list. That is
not a coincidence and not a finding — it is because none of the five books has
a notation section the concept extractor recognises, so the declared set is
empty and EVERY Greek letter in every equation is "undeclared".

So "112 undeclared math symbols" reads as a per-symbol defect count and is
really five books with no machine-readable notation section. The number is
correct and the phrasing invites a wrong reading, which is worth saying before
anyone quotes it.

THE 261 ACRONYMS ARE MOSTLY NOT ACRONYMS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Classified all 261 rather than sampled:

   initials or 2-letter tokens   122   AA, AC, AF, AG, AH, AJ, CW, DK, IP, VB
   citation keys                  56   BCF68, BCS10, BGM19, BM12, BMMS86, CJS95
   candidate REAL acronyms        57   AMS, NSF, ADM, AIP, DNA, ISBN, LU, LDU, CG
   place / address                14   MA, NJ, RI, CA, TX, USA, WA
   capitalised English words       8   OF, AND, OK, CO, SO, INPUT, OUTPUT
   cross-reference labels          4   Q2, R2, S1, V1

At most 57 of 261 (22%) are plausibly acronyms at all, and several of those
need no expansion in context — LU, LDU and CG are standard in a numerical
linear algebra text, and ISBN/ISSN come from the copyright page.

The two largest buckets are artefacts of WHERE the rule looks. It runs over the
whole document, so it sees the bibliography's author initials, the in-text
citation labels of a book that cites as [BCF68], the pseudocode keywords INPUT
and OUTPUT, and the publisher's address. My first hypothesis was that these
cluster in the bibliography; I checked and it is false — only 1% of
Geometrodynamics' 148 appear solely in the last 15% of pages. They are spread
through the body because that is where citation labels and pseudocode are.

WHAT I WOULD FIX, AND HAVE NOT
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Not fixed under this task, which asked for a report:

   * exclude citation-key-shaped tokens from acronym_undefined — the model
     already knows which strings are citations, from the bibliography pass.
     That alone removes 56 of 261.
   * exclude single- and two-letter tokens, which are 122 more and are almost
     never acronyms.
     Those two rules together take 261 to 83 without touching a real finding.
   * report symbol consistency as its own kind, with the frequency split, so
     the 24 singletons surface and the 31 deliberate ones do not.

Together those would take 1,487 gaps to roughly 1,250 and make the acronym and
symbol columns mean what their names say. citation_unmatched (1,061, 71% of
all gaps) is untouched by this and is its own question.
