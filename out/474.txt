THE REFERENCE CASE IS FOUND
---------------------------
  cardona-qft-methods_EQ0654, page 261, confidence 0.167

    ...\left(\frac{\overleftarrow{\delta}}{\partial x^{\mu}}
            +\frac{\overleftarrow{口}}{\partial y^{\mu}}\right)...

  term A  \frac{\overleftarrow{\delta}}{\partial x^{\mu}}
  term B  \frac{\overleftarrow{口}}{\partial y^{\mu}}
  symbol  \delta  vs  口 (U+53E3 CJK UNIFIED IDEOGRAPH)
  index   1 (x vs y)

THE DEFINITION, AND THE TWO THINGS IT HAD TO GET RIGHT
-------------------------------------------------------
The reference terms differ in TWO positions, not one: `\delta` vs `口` AND
`x` vs `y`. The second is what parallel terms are SUPPOSED to differ in. So
the rule separates the kinds of difference rather than counting them:

  expected   both single ASCII alphanumerics; both signs; both separators
  symbol     anything else — the signal

A pair qualifies with exactly ONE symbol difference.

Two corrections were needed and both changed the answer by an order of
magnitude:

  * Splitting the expression on `+` does not work. The reference `+` sits
    inside `e^{\frac{1}{2}\left(…\right)\theta^{\mu\nu}P_{\nu}}`, so a split
    leaves a `\frac{1}{2}\left(` prefix on the left and a `\right)\theta…`
    suffix on the right, and the two "terms" have different lengths. The
    window has to grow OUT from the operator. First attempt found 0 hits on
    the reference case.
  * Exempting only single ASCII LETTERS gave 50,837 pairs, of which 7,242
    were `1 vs 2` and 5,117 `1 vs 3`. `x_{1}+x_{2}` is a sum, not a defect.
    A digit varies for the same reason a subscript letter does. With digits,
    signs and table separators exempted: 17,212.

AND THAT IS THE FINDING, IN THE DIRECTION I DID NOT EXPECT
-----------------------------------------------------------
Exactly ONE of 17,212 involves a CJK token, and it is the reference case.

The rest are overwhelmingly correct mathematics. `\cos vs \sin` (557),
`\alpha vs \beta` (483), `^ vs _` (747), `\cdot vs \wedge` (134) — two
parallel terms differing in one symbol is what a textbook DOES. The
parallelism is a real structural signal and it is not, on its own, a defect
detector: it finds terms that differ, and an author differing on purpose
looks exactly like an OCR differing by accident.

What made EQ0654 findable was not the parallelism. It was that `口` is not a
mathematics symbol at all. The parallelism told you WHERE in the expression
to look; the Unicode block told you it was wrong.

So for the CJK class specifically, this adds nothing a Unicode check (475)
would not already find, and it costs a scan of 1.35 million values to say so.
That is worth knowing before building the pipeline on it.

WHAT WAS BUILT
--------------
  tools/parallel474.py   the scan. Reads `latex` off the projections — no
                         model, no rebuild, no measurement. 1,349 documents
                         in about 7 minutes.

The MAX_K = 60 bound on the window is why: the comparison cannot be made
incremental (growing k moves the left window's START, so every position
re-aligns), and unbounded it was a five-hour job. Written the slow way on
purpose, bounded instead, and the bound is documented beside the loop.

Nothing else was changed.
