Every distribution below is measured on 0902.0431 ALONE, and out/066 shows

The corpus figure disagrees with mine sharply. out/066 measured 3,392 clean

So corpus-wide roughly FOUR FIFTHS of clean rows sit BELOW the floor. On
0902.0431 I measured the opposite: 91.4% ABOVE it.

Both can be true — that is what an outlier is — but it means the conclusion
below ("the floor never fires, NOISE_COMP_DELTA is the binding gate") is
established for 0902.0431 and NOT established for the corpus. out/097's
sensitivity on the 49-document population says the floor DOES bite there:
floor 6 -> 724 flagged, floor 7 -> 591, a move of 18%.

I generalised from one document, which is the same error as setting a floor on
one document — the error this file was written to correct. The restatement
needs the pooled measurement it recommends at the end, and until that lands
the numbers below should be read as "0902.0431", not "the corpus".

THE HEADLINE: THE FLOOR OF 7 IS NOT THE BINDING GATE. Changing it changes
NOTHING. The threshold that decides almost every row is NOISE_COMP_DELTA = 2,
and it is wrong by about a factor of ten.

   conf >= 0.9 rows (n=105), classes under three different distance floors:

      floor  7   K=0  N=4  W=0  S=0  C=101
      floor 27   K=0  N=4  W=0  S=0  C=101
      floor 96   K=0  N=4  W=0  S=0  C=101

Identical. `flag_of` tests `comp_delta > 2` BEFORE it tests the distance, so
101 of 105 rows are classified `component` and never reach the floor at all.
The floor governs the 4 rows that survive the component test.

FIRST, THE PREMISE — TWO INSTRUMENTS, AND I MEASURED THE RIGHT ONE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The task names the distribution p50 39 / p90 141. That is out/184's CROP ink
distance — my own metric, |Δcomponents| + |Δholes| between a standalone render
and a scan crop. The floor of 7 does not govern it; the floor of 7 lives in
`fill_ink_fields.flag_of` and governs inkdrill's ROW-level five-tuple
(components, holes, stacked, centred, offset) measured from a finished report
page. Restating one against the other's distribution would be the exact
category error this thread keeps finding.

So I measured the row metric on its own terms — 0902.0431 rebuilt at
--min-conf 0.9 --no-legend, 20 pages, 105 rows, one page refused by the
coverage floor:

   ROW metric, clean high-confidence rows (n=105)
      distance        min 2   p10 9   p50 27   p90 96   max 324
      component delta         p50 13  p90 39   max 131
      rows at distance exactly 0:  0

   CROP metric, clean high-confidence rows (n=100, out/184)
      distance        min 0   p50 39   p90 141  max 638

The two instruments agree on the thing that matters: a row nobody doubts sits
at a distance in the tens, not below seven. 8.6% of clean rows fall at or
below 7 on the row metric; on the crop metric it was 13%. The conclusion is
the same whichever instrument is used, which is the strongest form this
argument can take.

EVERY GATE USING THE FLOOR, AND WHAT IT DOES
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   fill_ink_fields.py:58   NOISE_DISTANCE   = 7
   fill_ink_fields.py:59   NOISE_COMP_DELTA = 2
   fill_ink_fields.py:70   flag_of() -> K / N / W / S / C
   scratchpad remeasure/measure.py:21   the same two constants, copied

   consumers: every report.ink.json; the Residual column of every published
   report; the C/W/S/N/K legend; 6 of the 10 documents on the site.

There is no floor of 7 anywhere in pdfdrill's own source. `refine` has none —
it accepts on a strict fall, `after < before`, which needs no threshold.

TWO CLASSES ARE UNREACHABLE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   K (clean)  requires distance == 0.  Measured: 0 of 105 clean rows,
                                       0 of 55 doubted rows. 0 of 160.
   S (stable) requires scale-stability. Measured: 0 of 105, 0 of 55.

The legend on every published report advertises five classes and the data can
produce three. K and S have never been earned by any row I have measured, and
K cannot be: two independent rasterisations of the same page differ somewhere,
so an exact five-way tie does not occur.

That is the same defect as the legend that promised a residual column seven
documents did not have — a key naming something the artefact cannot contain —
and it is still there, one level down.

WHAT I AM NOT DOING, AND WHY
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
I am not changing the constants. Three reasons, in order of weight:

1. `fill_ink_fields.py` is not mine. It lives in ~/Downloads and belongs to the
   auditor's workflow; the constants are documented there as "inkdrill's
   measured floors (tools/findings.py) — do not invent new ones". Editing
   another party's calibration on my own evidence is exactly what that comment
   forbids, and it would be worse here because the numbers ARE published.

2. Changing them re-classes every residual bullet on the live site. Six
   documents, and the pages session runs a deploy gate that asserts the
   index's residual bars match the ink.json they describe. A re-class is an
   index change and an artefact change in the same commit, on their side.

3. My sweep rests on 160 rows from ONE document. The separation optimum at
   Δcomp 20 is a single number from a single book, and a threshold set on one
   document is how a floor of 7 came to be applied to a metric it was never
   measured against.

WHAT I RECOMMEND INSTEAD
   Measure the row metric on clean high-confidence rows across several
   documents — the harness is written and takes about four minutes a document
   — and set NOISE_COMP_DELTA from that pooled distribution. The distance
   floor can stay at 7 or move to 96; on this evidence it makes no difference
   to any row, and saying so is more useful than changing it.

   And whatever is chosen, K and S should be removed from the legend or given
   a definition a row can actually satisfy. A key that lists two classes
   nothing can earn is not a key, it is a claim.
