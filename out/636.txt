WHY IT HAPPENED — READ OFF THE DATA, NOT GUESSED
  MathPix puts EVERY footnote of a page into ONE `\footnotetext{...}` group.
  penev_A's page-4 block is one `footnote` line with three CHILD lines:
      c1  "\n\n\footnotetext{\n\({ }^{3}\) For a recent review ... 1996)."
      c2  "\n\({ }^{4}\) PCA has also been utilized, albeit implicitly, ..."
      c3  " described in Section 4.2, ... their first step.\n}"
  `FootnoteProcessor._parse_footnotetext` took the whole balanced group as ONE
  body, so footnote 4's printed number and its text were inside footnote 3.
  28 blocks hold 51 labels — 23 footnotes had no object of their own.

  THE SECOND CREATOR, WHICH THE BRIEF DID NOT NAME AND WHICH BLED AS BADLY.
  25 of the 53 are made by `heading_cleanup.extract_footnote_paragraphs`
  (`added_by: footnote_cleanup`) from a `\footnotetext{...}` MathPix left in a
  Paragraph. Split by creator, the 29 interior labels are 14 from the
  processor and 15 from the cleanup. Fixing only `footnote.py` would have left
  half the defect standing, so both call ONE splitter.

THE RULE — EXACT, NEVER A GUESS (src/docmodel/footnote_split.py)
  A footnote LABEL is `{ }^{n}` at the HEAD of an inline-math span (`\(` or
  `$`): a superscript with an EMPTY BASE, which is how a printed footnote
  number reaches MathPix. In `\(\sigma_{r}{ }^{2}\)` a base precedes the
  superscript, so it is not a label and is never a cut point — penev_A carries
  52 of those, and after this change the .tex holds exactly those 52 and no
  other `{ }^{n}` at all.
  A body runs from just after its own label to just before the NEXT label, or
  to the end of the block. The first body starts at the GROUP, not at its own
  label: text before the first number is a footnote spilling in from the
  previous page and the splitter invents no owner for it.
  A label whose number REPEATS one already emitted from the same block is
  refused — a second object would give two bodies one printed number on one
  page, the title collision 644 removed. The tail then stays on the earlier
  body, which is marked `tail_unassigned`, and is counted `orphan_tail`.
  ORPHAN_TAIL IS 0 ON BOTH DOCUMENTS. 28 penev_A blocks, 51 labels, no repeat;
  the refusal is exercised by a unit test, not by these two documents, and it
  is reported as 0 rather than left unstated.

  The body's own number is stripped exactly as bodies have always stripped it:
  only the CLOSED spelling `\({ }^{n}\)`. When the number shares its span with
  the body's maths (`\({ }^{21} \mathbf{K}^{2} \equiv ...\)`, two such blocks)
  nothing is removed — cutting inside that span would unbalance it.

THE CLAIM — AND THE TRADE, MEASURED BOTH WAYS AND WRITTEN DOWN
  646 sees the same defect from the anchor side. The brief asked for a
  sub-anchor so the claim is inline and the doubly-claimed anchors drop. BOTH
  arrangements were built and measured on the real document:

    (a) the footnote claims ONLY its label's line, inline
          doubly-claimed  42 -> 6   (all 36 Footnote pairs gone)
          unclaimed      243 -> 436 (+193)
        The 193 are lines whose ONLY claimant was a cleanup Footnote (two
        thirds of these paragraphs are pure footnote text). Their text is in
        a Footnote object; reporting them as "covered by no object" is loss
        that is not there. REFUSED.

    (b) the footnote claims its real EXTENT — the lines it owns WHOLE, plus
        an inline sub-anchor for a line it SHARES (prose before it, or the
        next number after it). `\footnotetext{` and whitespace are markup,
        not another object's content, so a line carrying only those before
        the label is still owned whole.
          doubly-claimed  42 -> 42  unclaimed 243 -> 243   TAKEN.

  SO THE 35 `Footnote+Paragraph` PAIRS DO NOT DROP, AND THE REASON IS
  MEASURED, NOT ASSUMED: `extract_footnote_paragraphs` narrows the
  paragraph's TEXT and never its REALIZATION, so the Paragraph still claims
  lines whose text it no longer holds. Narrowing it is what would drop them —
  and the TiddlyWiki projector rebuilds paragraph text FROM those lines, so
  that moves every paragraph tiddler on the document and takes the formulas
  hosted on those lines with it. That is 636-b, its own task with its own
  before/after. What this task did not do is buy the pair count with 193

CONCERNS
  93 Footnote objects on a document that prints far fewer is not a clean
  number, and it is not smoothed over: roughly half are second copies
  (646-b), which is 637's task. What 636 can say is that both copies now
  carry ONE footnote each instead of up to four merged.
  `orphan_tail` is 0 on both documents. The refusal it counts is real and
  unit-tested, but it did not fire here; a reader should treat it as a rule
  with a counter, not as a measured population.
  The `_footnote_extents` scan matches a group's numbers against the
  paragraph's lines in reading order. That is exact while the numbers appear
  in the lines in the same order as in the text, which held on all 25 penev_A
  paragraphs; a document where MathPix reorders them would fall back to the
  copied realization and be counted, not mis-anchored.
  Two new props reach the model — `split_index` (which body of its block this
  is) and `tail_unassigned` — and `docs/layers/PROPS.md` does not yet name
  them. That table is GENERATED from `corpus_props.json` (a walk of every
  model in the library) plus `props_code.json`; regenerating it here would
  fold in every other document's props and produce a diff that has nothing to
  do with 636. `python3 tools/propstable.py` after the next corpus walk picks
  them up. The contract tests are green as they stand.
  Both documents' models were rebuilt from CACHED MathPix output. No
  extraction was re-purchased and no `mathpix --force` was run.
