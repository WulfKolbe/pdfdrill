520 — the invented-\left. class, as its own defect
==================================================

Written for sending: docs/mathpix/2026-09-02-invented-left-dot.md

THE CLASS
---------
26 rows, 4 documents, 3 pdf_ids. A value that BEGINS with `\left.` and
carries a `\right\lrcorner`.

`\left.` is a null delimiter — it prints nothing, so it can never
correspond to anything on the page. Its only possible purpose is to
balance a `\right` that should not be there. A leading `\left.` beside a
`\right<corner>` is therefore self-evidently an invented fence.

The mechanism: the interior-product operator ⌟ (U+231F) is decoded as a
CLOSING DELIMITER instead of a binary operator. The bracket group then
moves — the maths changes — while the rendered picture stays nearly
identical.

VERIFIED AGAINST CROPS, NOT ASSERTED
-------------------------------------
Three rows read against their scans, in three different documents:

  mielke_EQ0294 p101   page prints ⌋ as a binary operator three times;
                       MathPix returns \downharpoonleft twice and
                       \right\lrcorner once. Same glyph, three readings.

  1-s2.0_EQ0015 p5     page prints  d*η = ⌟(∇η).
                       returned \left.d^{*}\eta=\right\lrcorner(\nabla\eta)

  Complex_..._EQ0851 p241  THE PROOF. The page prints Φ⌟∂_b v twice in one
                       line. MathPix returns the first as \Phi\lrcorner
                       \partial_{b} v — correct, bare operator — and the
                       second, eight tokens later, as \Phi\right\lrcorner
                       \partial_{b} v — a fence. Confidence 1.000.

That last row is why this is neither a glyph ambiguity nor a vocabulary
gap: the model had the right token and used it correctly in the same
expression. It is a decoding decision that flipped mid-expression. Ten of
the 26 needed TWO invented `\left.` to stay balanced.

WHY IT IS NEW TO THEM
----------------------
The eight already sent are wrong SYMBOLS (CJK, \nvdash, l-for-1, dropped
cells). This is a wrong ARITY: the symbol is right and its role is wrong.
Every existing check on both sides misses it —

  it compiles                       0 xelatex errors
  their confidence is high          median 1.000
  our ink cannot see it             all measured rows read as agreement

A grouping error is a small-ink error. 465 already established that ink
plus high confidence is a rendering-difference detector; here there is
not even a rendering difference to detect.

The structural check is nearly free: `^\left.` together with a
`\right<corner>` fires 26 times in 634,258 equations across 1,349
documents.
