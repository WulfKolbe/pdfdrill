THE ANSWER TO THE QUESTION AS PUT

  "If the new build's cell has pixels in the 100–200 range and the published
  one has pixels below 50, the crops are being rendered lighter."

  The opposite holds. The new build has ZERO pixels in 100–200 and 2,334
  below 50; the published one has both. Nothing is lighter, and a threshold
  of 200 catches ink in both.

  "If both look the same, the extraction is at fault and it's inkdrill's."

  Both have ink below 50 in the same proportion. THE EXTRACTION IS AT FAULT.
  It is not a threshold constant.

WHERE THE SCAN CROP CAME FROM

  Both builds take it from the same place: a local file, via
  \includegraphics[width=120.0mm]{report-crops/2501.06662_EQ0051.jpg}.
  PDFDRILL_CDN_BASE is UNSET, so no drop-in source is redirecting it.

  BUT THE FILES CHANGED. Of the 445 crops in report-crops/, 60 date from
  2026-08-24 — the era of the published build — and 385 were REWRITTEN on
  2026-09-03, during my sweeps. So the source did change between the published
  build and now.

  What it changed INTO is the opposite of lighter: the new raster has no
  midtones at all (0 pixels in 50–229) where the published one has a small
  antialiasing tail. Harder, not fainter. That is consistent with a re-fetch
  at a different scale or resampling, and it does not produce an empty cell —
  the ink is there and darker.

  I am flagging the 385 rewritten crops separately: a CDN crop fetch is a
  network step, and I did not ask before those runs went and got them.

CONCLUSION

  The crops are not lighter, the ink is present at the same density in both,
  and the threshold would catch it either way. Every data row still reads
  R=[0,0,0,0,0] while the header row on the same page reads R=[10,4,1,1,0]
  and the lattice reports the correct 6x6 geometry.

  It is inkdrill's cell extraction. Nothing in pdfdrill should be changed on
  the strength of this measurement, and I have changed nothing.
