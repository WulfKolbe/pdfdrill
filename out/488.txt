So 487 is not three joins. It is one join, one lookup and a sort — but two of
the three are missing often enough that the prompt has to work without them.

1 — THE ABSTRACT
----------------
35 of 52 sampled documents carry an Abstract object; 17 do not. johnston has
ZERO — it is a textbook, and the six test-bed rows 487 would run on are in a
document with no abstract at all.

That matters for the test: whichever way 487 comes out on those six rows, the
abstract half of the context will not have been exercised. The first section's
text is the available substitute there, and it is a different thing.

2 — THE ENCLOSING SECTION
-------------------------
`parent_section` holds a Section object id, and the Section carries `caption`
and `level`. On johnston every one of 6,813 math objects resolves:

  parent_section 'obj_a3c0094cd391' -> {'level': '1',
                                        'caption': 'Introduction to Linear and Matrix'}

Corpus-wide it resolves for 69.6% of math objects. The level it points at:

  level 1  2,472    level 2  1,675    level 3  424    level 4  4

So it is a real enclosing section and not always the root — but on johnston
the samples all point at level 1, which is the book title. A prompt that says
"the enclosing section is 'Introduction to Linear and Matrix'" is telling the
model the name of the book. Worth knowing before it is written.

Six of 52 documents have no section structure on their math at all. The
prompt must omit the line rather than emit an empty one.

3 — THE K NEAREST FORMULAS
--------------------------
The cheapest of the three: `flow_index` is present on 100% of math objects in
every document sampled, and `page` on 95.4%. Nearest-by-flow needs no join
and no computation — sort the document's math objects by flow_index and take
the k on either side. Nearest-by-page needs `page`, which 2010.06467 (344
math objects) does not have.

Recommended: order by flow_index and report the page when there is one, since
flow_index never fails and page does.
