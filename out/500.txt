500 — why 1511.08771's model is 4.96 GB
2026-09-02T15:10Z · measured by streaming; parsing it costs 16.4 GB and does not finish

THE ANSWER: IT IS NOT THE OBJECTS. IT IS THE PER-CHARACTER ANCHOR STREAM.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
The top-level sections, located by byte offset without parsing:

  section        first byte        size      share
  ---------------------------------------------------------
  meta                    4      ~1 MB       0.0%
  streams           974,225    4.06 GB      81.8%
  objects     4,062,376,991    0.85 GB      17.2%
  alignments  4,915,972,733      44 MB       0.9%

**`streams` is 4.06 GB, 82% of the file.** Every object in the document — all
680,000 of them — accounts for 0.85 GB.

WHAT IS IN IT
-------------
One anchor per CHARACTER. A slice from the middle reads

  "a_d66d3e7f1b19",
  "a_8d3e2b265cb8",
  "a_3a49fa1fbe6e",  …

and counted over the whole file:

  "codepoint"           30,605,554
  "a_…" references      65,757,148      2.1 per codepoint
  "anchors" arrays          94,426
  "payload" blocks          94,426

  30.6 million codepoints over 5,000 pages = 6,121 characters per page
  4.06 GB / 30.6M codepoints = 132.7 bytes per character

So the model stores 30.6 million character anchors, each referenced about
twice — once where it is defined with its codepoint, once in an `anchors`
array — and each reference is a 14-character id written as
`        "a_xxxxxxxxxxxx",\n`. At `indent=1` that is ~26 bytes for a 14-byte
identifier; compact separators would be ~17.

This is the documented design — "anchor-based `Stream`s" — behaving exactly as
designed on a document with 5,000 pages. It is not a defect of this document.
It is the cost of the design multiplied by an unusual page count, and nothing
else in the corpus comes close: the next largest e-print model is 0.09 GB.

The cells dominate the object count, and the objects are 17% of the file.
Even removing every cell would not change the order of magnitude.

FORMULA OBJECTS INSIDE A TABLE CELL: ZERO, AND THE QUESTION IS MIS-POSED
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Corpus-wide over 1,355 models (1511.08771 skipped by the 499 size guard):

  documents with a Formula whose parent chain reaches a TableCell:   0

And the reason is structural, checked on penev_A before the zero was
believed:

  TableCell objects       54, all with a parent, NONE with children
  parents of TableCells   Table, 54 of 54
  parents of Formulas     (none) — 674 of 674 have NO parent at all

A cell's content lives in `props.text`, not in child objects, and Formulas
are not nested under anything. "A Formula inside a table cell" is not a
relation this model can express, so the count is zero for a reason that has
nothing to do with tables.

A Formula could still sit inside a table's REGION geometrically. That is a
different question, answerable by region overlap, and it is not the one that
was asked.

DISTINCT CONTENT AGAINST TOTAL: INTERNING IS NOT THE MECHANISM EITHER
----------------------------------------------------------------------
  key           occurrences      distinct     ratio
  -------------------------------------------------
  latex              71,170        70,999     1.002
  latex_code            103             1       103
  text            2,098,421       493,926     4.25

The hypothesis was a few hundred distinct strings referenced tens of
thousands of times. `latex` is 99.8% distinct — 70,999 different strings in
71,170 occurrences. `text` repeats 4.25 times on average, which for a
document of 6,091 tables is unremarkable (a column of "0" and "1" repeats).

Interning would save something on `text` and almost nothing on `latex`, and
neither touches the 82% that is the anchor stream. The docpack — which IS
string-interned — is 1.55 GB against the model's 4.96 GB, a 3.2x saving, and
that is the measured ceiling on what interning buys here.

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR 501
-----------------------
501 asks what changes if a table cell's content is a cell rather than a
Formula. On this document the answer is: **almost nothing**. There are no
Formulas in cells to reclassify, the cells are already TableCell objects, and
the objects are 17% of a file whose bulk is the character stream.

The rule may still be right for other reasons, but it will not make this
model smaller, and 501 should be answered against that rather than against
the 4.96 GB.

Nothing was changed.
