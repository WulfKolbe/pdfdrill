GAP 1 — code -> CodeListing
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE   44,689 lines, 205 documents.
         43,933 are children of a `diagram`; DiagramProcessor joins its
         children and pulls a fenced block out, recovering 43,553 as a string
         prop. Genuinely unreachable: 1,136 lines —
             380 under 51 diagrams whose joined text is not fenced, so
                 _extract_code returns None and the listing is treated as a
                 graphic;
             502 under a `column` parent, 241 with no parent, 8 under
                 `list_item`, 5 under `chart` — no processor looks at them.
         And a listing that exists only as a prop has no anchors: nothing can
         point at a line of it.

AFTER    6,243 CodeListing objects over 44,689 lines in 205 documents.
         0 runs produced nothing.
         Lines newly reachable at all: 1,136. Lines newly ADDRESSABLE (their
         own anchors, a surface realization): all 44,689.

HOW      One object per maximal run of consecutive `code` lines on a page,
         taking each line's own text verbatim and joining in order. It does not
         re-parse a fence to recover a body MathPix already handed over line by
         line. Fence lines are MathPix's delimiters and are dropped; the info
         string on an opening fence supplies `language` when it has one.
         Indentation is content and survives; one leading "\n" per line is
         MathPix's terminator and is stripped, so a genuinely blank interior
         line still survives (tested).

         The Diagram `code` prop is untouched — consumers depend on it, and a
         CodeListing realizes the code CHILD anchors while the Diagram realizes
         the diagram line, so no anchor is claimed twice.

         `code` was also missing from ParagraphProcessor's break types, exactly
         as `list_item` was in 248: a listing did not end the prose run it
         interrupted. Added.

FALLBACK n/a — there is no heuristic here. The field IS the content.


GAP 2 — cnt, the true quadrilateral
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE   3,465,341 lines carry `cnt`; every crop used `region`.

         Not axis-aligned:                     4,886 lines, 152 documents
           ...where region != the polygon bbox 4,618
           ...where region IS the bbox            268
         Axis-aligned:                     3,459,944
           ...region identical to the bbox  3,158,114
           ...differing by 0-2px               22,655 (inclusive-bound padding)

         So on upright lines the two agree to within a pixel or two, and there
         was nothing to win. On rotated lines a rectangle drawn round slanted
         text is wider than the text: bbox area / polygon area is 1.06x at the
         median, 1.28x at p90, 1.46x at p99 and 147x at worst. arXiv 1808.07302
         has diagonal annotations inflating 3.7x-5.6x.

AFTER    4,618 crop rectangles changed, in 151 documents.
         105 of them on an image-bearing line (diagram / chart / molecule).
         4,886 quadrilaterals carried onto Diagram / Picture as `quad`.

HOW      `mathpix.crop_region(payload)` returns `region` unless `cnt` says the
         line is rotated AND the polygon's own bbox is strictly tighter. A CDN
         crop is a rectangle, so the polygon cannot make the crop
         non-rectangular — it can only make it tight, and be carried so a
         consumer that wants to mask or deskew has the corners.

         `quad` is stored ONLY when it is not an upright rectangle. Storing it
         on all 3.4M lines would restate `region` and bloat every model file
         for nothing.

FALLBACK 534,474 corpus lines have no `cnt` at all and keep `region`
         unchanged, as do all 3,459,944 upright ones — 99.87% of lines take the
         pre-259 path exactly.


GAP 3 — font_size -> header level
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE   43,160 section_header lines.
           with a \section-family command    9,032  (21%)
           without                          34,128  (79%)  ALL level 1

         Four fifths of every heading tree in the corpus was flat, and MathPix
         states a pixel height on every one of those lines.

AFTER    34,128 headers take their level from font_size, in 975 documents.
           level 1   6,478
           level 2   5,514
           level 3   5,166
           level 4   5,566
           level 5  11,404
         9,032 keep the LaTeX command. 0 fell through to the default.

HOW      Ranked WITHIN each document: distinct header sizes sorted largest
         first, rank = level. No threshold and no cross-document constant — a
         31px heading is a chapter title in one paper and a running subsection
         in another, so an absolute cut would be a constant with no population
         behind it.

FALLBACK The LaTeX command still wins wherever it exists — it is the author's
         own statement of depth, and a font size is an inference from it. A
         header with neither keeps level 1, and `level_basis` on every Section
         says which of the three decided.

CAVEAT   Levels are capped at 5, matching _LEVEL's own range, so a document
         with more than 5 distinct header sizes collapses its tail into level
         5. That is 83 documents (7%); 93% have 5 or fewer. The level-5 bucket
         is large (11,404) because those 83 are books with many headings.


GAP 4 — table_split_cell, and molecule
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE   table_split_cell: 10 lines, 7 documents, not in table.py's
         _CELL_TYPES. The `\backslashbox{Publication Type}{Language}` corner
         cell of a cross-tabulation, dropped — so the table read as though it
         had no row/column heading at all.

AFTER    10 cells collected in 7 documents. _CELL_TYPES is now
         {simple_cell, complex_cell, table_spanning_cell, table_split_cell}.

         Checked before trusting the one-line fix: all 10 are DIRECT children
         of the `table` and listed in its children_ids. `_collect_children`
         does not recurse, so had they hung off a `table_row` the change would
         have compiled, passed a green suite and collected nothing. My first
         test fixture nested them under a row and failed for exactly that
         reason.

         molecule: 0 changed. It was never a gap — see the top of this report.
         Named explicitly in picture.py as `_COVERED_BY_INLINE` so the next
         reader can see it is handled without having to reason about which
         branch a catch-all takes.
