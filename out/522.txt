522 — the low_agreement systematic error, diagnosed
===================================================

Population: the 1,112 `tex` candidates now on the 7 published documents
(844 injected by 521, 268 already on 1510.06699).

THE COUNTS
----------
`low_agreement` fires at mean agreement < 0.75 (scoring.py).

  document      candidates  median  low_agreement   as recorded
  0707.4470             53   0.971              1         1.9%
  0902.0431            649   0.882            123        19.0%
  1510.06699           268   0.919             20         7.5%
  1605.05775            26   0.968              1         3.8%
  2010.14265            13   0.797              5        38.5%
  2106.07890            46   0.996              7        15.2%
  2501.06662            57   0.929              3         5.3%
  ------------------------------------------------------------
  ALL                1,112   0.907            160        14.4%

It is NOT a systematic disagreement across the board — 86% agree at 0.75
or better and the median is 0.907. It is concentrated: 0902.0431 carries
123 of the 160.

There are THREE causes, and the first one is ours.

CAUSE 1 — THE COMPARISON IS WRONG. difflib autojunk.
-----------------------------------------------------
`latex_similarity` called `difflib.SequenceMatcher(None, na, nb)` with the
default `autojunk=True`. That heuristic marks every element occurring in
more than 1% of the SECOND sequence as "popular" and refuses to match on
it — but only once that sequence reaches 200 elements.

Normalized LaTeX has a tiny alphabet, so `\ _ = , - + 1 2 3 a b c` all
cross 1% at once and the ratio collapses.

Two consequences, both real:

  THE FUNCTION WAS NOT SYMMETRIC. On one 0902.0431 pair:

      latex_similarity(candidate, mathpix) = 0.857   <- injectlatex's order
      latex_similarity(mathpix, candidate) = 0.005   <- the same two strings
      with autojunk=False, either order  = 0.857

  0.857 is the true value; 0.005 is the artefact. The normalized candidate
  is 231 characters and the normalized MathPix reading is 175 — one side of
  200, one side of the other.

  IT WAS ACTIVE FOR 91 OF THE 1,112 SCORES (8.2%), every one a long
  multi-line equation: exactly the population where the reading is hardest
  and the score matters most.

Fixed (`autojunk=False`), low_agreement falls 160 -> 125, i.e. 35 of the
160 were the metric libelling a good match.

  document      low as recorded   low with the fix
  0707.4470                   1                  0
  0902.0431                 123                105
  1510.06699                 20                  6
  1605.05775                  1                  1
  2010.14265                  5                  5
  2106.07890                  7                  6
  2501.06662                  3                  2
  ----------------------------------------------
  ALL                       160                125  (14.4% -> 11.2%)

THE JOIN IS NOT WRONG
---------------------
Checked directly, because a depressed score could in principle pick the
wrong equation. For all 45 autojunk-affected candidates in 0902.0431,
recomputing the argmax over every equation in the document with the fixed
metric attaches each candidate to THE SAME OBJECT it already has. Zero
would move.

The reason is that the corruption depresses the correct match and its
competitors together, so the ordering survives. What it CAN do is push a
correct match below injectlatex's 0.55 attach threshold, which silently
drops it — a false negative, not a mis-join. 0902.0431 attached 649 of
1,197 source equations (54%); how many of the other 548 were lost this way
is not knowable without re-injecting under the fixed metric.

CAUSE 2 — THE AUTHOR'S MACROS ARE UNEXPANDED (the third hypothesis, and
it is the largest)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
Of the 125 rows still below 0.75 after the fix:

  unexpanded \es / \J style macros    61   49%
  plain-TeX \cr                       33   26%
  \pmatrix                            33   26%
  \!\!  (spacing)                     18   14%
  \vspace                             17   14%
  \qquad, \;                          13   10%
  eqnarray                             2    2%

`expand_macros` found 138 macro definitions in 0902.0431 and still left
`\es`, `\J`, `\so` unexpanded in the attached candidates, so the comparison
is between MathPix's expanded `\mathfrak{D}_{4}` and the author's
`\text{\es {D}}_4`. Those are the same mathematics and score 0.612.

CAUSE 3 — THE NORMALIZER DOES NOT FOLD PLAIN TeX
-------------------------------------------------
`normalize_latex` strips `\left`, `\right`, `&`, `\\` and `\begin{aligned}`
but knows nothing of `\pmatrix{...\cr...}`, `\vspace`, `\Big`, `\!\!\!` or
`\qquad`. A 1990s plain-TeX source therefore scores low against MathPix's
`\begin{array}` output for reasons that are purely notational.

THE THREE EXAMPLES, BOTH READINGS
----------------------------------
A — the metric is broken. recorded 0.857 | reversed 0.005 | fixed 0.857

  MathPix \begin{aligned} \lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2} & =\alpha_{1}, &
          \lambda_{1}-\lambda_{3} & =2 \alpha_{1}+3 \alpha_{2}, ...
  author  \begin{array}{cccc} \lambda_1 - \lambda_2 = \alpha_1, & \;\;\;
          \lambda_1 - \lambda_3 = 2\alpha_1 + 3\alpha_2, ...

  The same equation. `aligned` vs `array`, spacing macros, and subscript
  braces — nothing else.

B — the author's macros are unexpanded. 0.612 in every order.

  MathPix \mathfrak{D}_{4}=\mathfrak{s o}(8)=\mathfrak{s o}(\mathfrak{C})
          =\left\{D \in \operatorname{Hom}_{\boldsymbol{R}}(\mathfrak{C})
          \mid(D x, y)+(x, D y)=0
  author  \text{\es {D}}_4 = \text{\es {so}}(8) = \text{\es {so}}(\text{\es
          {C}}) = \{ D \in \text\mathrm{{Hom}}_{\text{${R}$}}(\text{\es {C}})
          \, | \, (Dx, y) ...

  MathPix is RIGHT and the author's side is unexpanded. `\es` is the
  author's own macro for a fraktur alphabet.

C — plain-TeX markup. 0.724.

  MathPix \mathfrak{h}=\left\{\left.\left(\begin{array}{ccc} \lambda_{1} & 0
          & 0 \\ 0 & \lambda_{2} & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & \lambda_{3}
          \end{array}\right) \right\rvert\, ...
  author  \text{\es {h}} = \Big\{\pmatrix{\lambda_1 & 0 & 0 \cr 0 &
          \lambda_2 & 0 \cr 0 & 0 & \lambda_3} \Big| \, \lambda_i \in ...

  Identical mathematics; `\pmatrix{...\cr}` against `\begin{array}` and
  `\Big\{` against `\left\{`.

WHAT WAS CHANGED, AND WHAT WAS NOT
-----------------------------------
CHANGED: `latex_similarity` now passes `autojunk=False`, with the reason
written at the call site and two tests — one asserting symmetry on a
>200-character pair, one that identical strings score 1.0. 2,915 passing.

NOT CHANGED, deliberately: nothing was re-injected and nothing rebuilt.
The 1,112 candidates on disk were joined under the broken metric. The join
survives it, so compare.html is not showing the wrong equations — but the
SCORES it displays are wrong for 91 rows, and an unknown number of source
equations were never attached because their score fell under 0.55.

WHAT 523/524 SHOULD NOT ASSUME
-------------------------------
`low_agreement` is not currently a defect signal. After the metric fix it
is 125 rows, and at least 61 of those are the author's own macros left
unexpanded — a fault on our side of the comparison, not a reading error by
either party. Until `expand_macros` covers `\def`-style definitions and
`normalize_latex` folds plain TeX, a low agreement score means "these two
strings are spelled differently", not "one of them is wrong".

NEXT, NOT DONE HERE
-------------------
1. Re-inject the 7 documents under the fixed metric and report how many
   MORE source equations attach (0902.0431 is at 54%).
2. Teach `expand_macros` the `\def` forms it is missing.
3. Fold `\pmatrix`/`\cr`/`\vspace`/spacing in `normalize_latex`.
Each changes the number, so none of them should be done inside a rebuild.
