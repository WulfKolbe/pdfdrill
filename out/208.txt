208 — the Mielke \nvdash case: it is a slashed L, and the same glyph is
      also read as Z in the very next equation
==============================================================================
VERDICT: a MathPix defect of the same class as the CJK ideographs — a glyph
rendered by a semantically wrong command. Confirmed by looking at the crop,
not by inference.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   bibkey       Geometrodynamics of Gauge Fields (Eckehard W. Mielke)

   LaTeX as MathPix emitted it:
      \nvdash^{A}{ }_{B}:=L^{A}{ }_{B}-\frac{1}{n} \delta_{B}^{A} L^{C}{ }_{C} .

   crop

WHAT THE CROP SHOWS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   Ⱡ^{A}{}_{B} := L^{A}{}_{B} - (1/n) δ^{A}_{B} L^{C}{}_{C}

An L with a diagonal stroke through it. Not a turnstile: no vertical bar with
a detached horizontal arm, and it carries an upper and a lower index, which a
turnstile never does.

THE SURROUNDING TEXT NAMES IT
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The paragraph immediately before, at flow index 3590 on the same page:

   "In general, the metalinear group SL(n+1, ℝ) with det g = 1 is generated by
    n(n+2) TRACE-FREE GENERATORS"

and the equation is precisely the trace-free part of L — L minus its trace
spread over the identity. So the slash means traceless, the text says
"trace-free", and the definition does what the text says. Three independent
confirmations of one reading.

THE PART I DID NOT EXPECT: THE SAME GLYPH IS ALSO READ AS Z
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The next equation, EQ0874, same page, confidence 0.990:

   emitted   \left[Z^{A}{ }_{B}, Z^{C}{ }_{D}\right]
             =\delta_{D}^{A} Z^{C}{ }_{B}-\delta_{B}^{C} Z^{A}{ }_{D}

   crop      [Ⱡ^{A}{}_{B}, Ⱡ^{C}{}_{D}] = δ^{A}_{D} Ⱡ^{C}{}_{B} − δ^{C}_{B} Ⱡ^{A}{}_{D}

The identical slashed L, four times, transcribed as a plain Z. Its own text
says "THEIR commutation relations" — referring to the generators just defined
— so the two equations are about the same object, and MathPix gave it two
different wrong names in consecutive lines, both at high confidence.

Counting the readings of that ^{A}{ }_{B}-indexed generator in this document:

   \nvdash   1   EQ0873, conf 0.9995   the definition's left-hand side
   Z         4   EQ0874, conf 0.9902   the commutation relations
   L         2   EQ0873, conf 0.9995   the genuine UNSLASHED L on the right

The last line matters: within one equation MathPix read the plain L correctly
as L and the slashed L as \nvdash. It DISTINGUISHED the two glyphs. It simply
had no name for the second and reached for one that looks like it.

THE SECOND TURNSTILE IN THIS DOCUMENT IS THE SAME STORY
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   Geometrodynamics_EQ0850, p275, confidence 0.032

Its crop is a page of Dirac slash notation — D̸², 𝒜̸, γ₅(D̸ 𝒜̸) — and the
\nVdash appears among them. A text with sixteen \not-constructions and no
logic operator at all is not a text about forcing.

SAME CLASS AS THE CJK IDEOGRAPHS, BUT A HARDER CASE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
out/190 listed two CJK defects: 久 and 匕 standing where operators belong. The
defect here is the same shape — a glyph produced by a command whose meaning is
unrelated to it — but it is worse in one respect and better in none:

   久 in an equation is ABSURD. Any reader sees it.
   ⊬ in a gauge-gravity text is PLAUSIBLE. It is a real mathematical symbol,
     in a real font, in a document that could conceivably discuss derivability.
     Nothing about the rendered page looks wrong.

And no structural check reaches it. EQ0873 compiles, its environments balance,
its table widths are uniform, it has no CJK and no confusable cell. It passes
every filter in out/187-189 and sits at confidence 0.9995. The only thing that
finds it is putting the crop beside the LaTeX and looking.

That makes it the first defect in this sequence discovered by READING rather
than by measuring, and it is worth saying plainly: the five in out/190 were
found because a structural check flagged them. This one was found because
out/207 asked why a turnstile had indices.

   1901.06950               p2    X_i \nVdash X_j | Z    inferred: conditional
                                  independence (⫫), not a turnstile
   Schaums Discrete Maths   p40   a \nVdash b        inferred: "a does not
                                  divide b" (\nmid)
   Geometrie und GPS        p45   inside a binary-addition array   inferred
   0707.4470                p24   inside a finite-difference quotient  inferred

Only the Mielke pair is confirmed against its crop; the other four are
inferred from context and are marked as such. Three of them are INLINE
formulas, which carry no CDN crop, so there is nothing to look at without
re-cropping the region myself — which is a task, not an aside.

If those inferences hold, five of the six uses of a negated turnstile in this
corpus are misreads of an entirely different symbol, and the sixth is a table
of symbols listing it deliberately.
