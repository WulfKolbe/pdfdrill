THE THREE CHANGES

  1. THE HEADER RULE. \noalign{\pdrulepos{hdr<table>p\thepage}} sits
     immediately after the header's \hline, inside \endhead. Each repetition
     defines its own label because \thepage expands at shipout. Read back per
     page, and a table's first row on each page takes it as rule_above_bp in
     place of the table's top rule.

     The label carries the TABLE ORDINAL as well as the page. A bare
     hdrule<page> would collide the moment two tables put a header on one
     page, which 0049 does not do and a longer document will.

  2. CENTRELINES, WITH THE SIGN DERIVED. Every \noalign savepos now sits
     AFTER its \hline, so every one of them records the rule's LOWER edge and
     one sign serves them all: + \arrayrulewidth/2. Getting there meant
     MOVING the row marks — they used to be emitted before their \hline, on
     the upper edge, which is why 612 needed a per-mark sign and left a
     -0.399 outlier when one mark sat on the other side. The special case is
     gone.

  3. COLUMN RULES TOO — and these keep a derived sign, because they straddle
     their rules rather than sitting on one side of them. A column mark
     kerned by \tabcolsep lands on the rule's INNER edge, so the left rules
     are half a rule high and the rightmost half a rule low. A constant
     applied to all of them would be wrong for the last one in every table.
