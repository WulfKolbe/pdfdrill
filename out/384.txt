WHAT 442 IS

  Equations whose `confidence` prop is strictly below 0.1, over the corpus
  capture out/324b.jsonl: every equation with confidence < 0.3 across 1,349
  models, 1,401 rows, one model skipped as too large to load.

    conf <  0.1      442     in 112 documents
    conf <= 0.1      442     identical — no equation has confidence exactly 0.1

  So the `<` / `<=` question, which is the usual source of an off-by-a-few,
  does not arise here. The two conventions agree.

EVERY OTHER CONVENTION I COULD CONSTRUCT

    conf <  0.1                                442
    conf <= 0.1                                442
    conf <  0.1, excluding exact 0.0           440   (2 rows are exactly 0)
    collapsing "(N)" duplicate folders         440   (3 base names duplicated)
    collapsing identical latex+confidence      433
    conf <  0.10 after rounding to 2 dp        419
    conf <  0.1  after rounding to 3 dp        438

  None yields 429. The nearest are 433 and 438, and neither is a defensible
  rule — collapsing identical LaTeX merges two genuinely distinct objects in
  two documents, and rounding before a threshold is simply wrong: it moves
  0.104 below 0.10 and 0.096 above it, changing membership by the display
  format rather than by the value.

WHICH IS CORRECT

  The exact stored value with a strict `<`. Confidence is a float MathPix
  emits; rounding is a presentation choice and must not decide a population.
  On that rule the answer is 442, and `<=` gives the same number.

WHERE 429 CAME FROM

  Not from any report in this repository — `429` appears in out/021.txt as a
  page count, in out/066.txt as a row index, in out/121.txt as an HTTP status
  and as the value 0.429, and nowhere as a count of low-confidence equations.

  It reached me in the task text of 381 and I used it there without checking,
  then measured 442 and reported the disagreement rather than the number. The
  reconciliation is that there is nothing to reconcile: 429 has no derivation
  I can find, and the only figure with one is 442.

  Reported as unresolved rather than forced. Two numbers agreeing after one
  of them is bent to fit is worse than two numbers differing.
