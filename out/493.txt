493 — can the TOC supply a section path for a book?
2026-09-02T17:15Z · the nine published non-arXiv books

THE Toc OBJECT, READ BEFORE ANYTHING WAS PROPOSED
--------------------------------------------------
  keys        children, id, parent, props, realizations, type
  props       bibkey, entries, flow_index, next_in_flow, prev_in_flow,
              parent_section
  children    0
  realizations one surface span over the mathpix_lines stream

`entries` is a FLAT LIST OF STRINGS, and one printed line produces three of
them — the whole line and both of its halves:

  '1. Introduction  ..... 2'
  '1. Introduction'
  '..... 2'
  '2. Almost finitely generated \(\mathcal{O}\)-mo…'

There is no title field, no locator field, no level field and no child
structure. Everything below has to be recovered from those strings.

1 — TITLE AND LOCATOR: PRESENT, AS TEXT, AT A LOW YIELD.
    All nine books carry exactly one Toc object. Eight of nine yield
    (number, title, page) triples under a strict parse; cardona yields ZERO
    because its entries are unnumbered and paginated in roman —
    'Introduction  ..... v', 'Part A LECTURES  ..... 1'.

    The yield is low even where it works: johnston has 1,172 entries and 55
    parses (4.7%), because the triple-emission above means most strings are
    fragments. lyche is the best at 290 of 1,020 (28%).

2 — THE PRINTED-TO-PDF OFFSET IS NOT STORED, AND DERIVING IT DOES NOT WORK.
    No meta key holds one. The meta keys across these books are authors,
    bibkey, bibkey_history, folder_history, geometry, num_pages, pages,
    publisher, root_id, source, source_path, title, year — nothing
    offset-like, nothing page-label-like.

    So it must be derived, and I tried: match each parsed TOC title against a
    Section object's caption, and take the mode of (Section's PDF page −
    TOC's printed page).

      book        parsed   Sections with a page   matched   offset modes
      lyche          290                     64        22   9 (x3), 20, 15
      mielke         147                     54        21   16 (x2), 13 (x2)
      voloshin        74                      6         3   −151, −171
      cardona          0                     24         0   —

    Three votes for an offset of 9 against single votes for 20 and 15 is not
    a measurement, it is noise. voloshin's −151 is a title matching the wrong
    section entirely. The Section objects that carry a page are far too few
    (6 to 64) against hundreds of TOC entries for the match to be anything
    but sparse and fuzzy.

3 — LEVELS: YES, FROM THE LOCATOR. NOT FROM INDENTATION.
    The dotted number gives the depth directly — 3 is level 1, 3.4 is level
    2 — and it works wherever the entry parses. lyche resolves to
    {1: 16, 2: 125, 3: 149}, seven-sketches to {1: 7, 2: 40, 3: 99}. This is
    the one part that is straightforwardly available, and it is exactly the
    part 492 found missing from parent_section, which is flat.

    Indentation cannot be used: the strings are stripped, and the
    three-way emission destroys the original line geometry anyway.

COVERAGE: FOR WHAT FRACTION OF MATH OBJECTS DOES THE PATH RESOLVE?
------------------------------------------------------------------
  Zero, as things stand.

Not because the TOC is unusable in principle — the levels are right there —
but because a path needs THREE things and only one of them is available:

  the depth       available     from the dotted locator
  the title       available     from the parsed entry
  WHERE IT STARTS unavailable   the printed page is known, the PDF page is
                                not, and the offset that converts one to the
                                other is neither stored nor derivable from
                                what is on disk

Without the offset there is no way to say which TOC entry encloses a math
object on PDF page 214. The 34,623 math objects across these nine books get
no path.

WHAT WOULD HAVE TO EXIST
------------------------
One number per book, and it is obtainable but not from the model:

  the PDF page on which printed page 1 falls. `pdfinfo`/pypdf expose PDF page
  LABELS where a publisher set them, and pdfdrill already reads the
  annotation layer for `links` and `dests` — a `dests` entry for a chapter is
  a PDF page number for a named destination, which is the same conversion
  from the other side.

That is a measurement to run, not a model to rebuild, and it is the whole
of what stands between the TOC and a working section path.

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR 487 AND 494
-------------------------------
492 established that parent_section gives papers nothing (0.32% of rows at
level >= 2) and 488 that it gives johnston the book's own title. 493
establishes that the TOC gives the DEPTH a section path needs and cannot
place it on a page.

So 494's rule — "from parent_section where it resolves below level 1, from
the TOC where the document is a book, omitted where neither gives anything" —
currently reduces to "omitted", for every document measured. The combined
coverage against 488's 69.6% is 69.6%: the TOC adds nothing until the offset
exists.

487 still waits, and now on a smaller and more specific thing than "the
context work": one number per book.
