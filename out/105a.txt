105a - row-width validation on the change-request loader
==============================================================================

WHICH OF THE FOUR FAIL: ONE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   0902.0431_EQ0515   PASS    6 rows, width 6
   0902.0431_EQ0516   PASS    5 rows, width 6
   0902.0431_EQ0526   PASS    4 rows, width 8
   0902.0431_EQ0517   FAIL   12 rows; widths {6:8, 5:2, 4:1, 2:1}, modal 6
                             non-uniform at rows 7, 8, 11, 12

   EQ0517's offending rows:
     row  7   5 entries   ...&= 1\ 1\ 0\ 1\ 0
     row  8   5 entries   ...&= 0\ 1\ 0\ 1\ 0
     row 11   4 entries   ...&= 1\ 2\ 3\ 2
     row 12   2 entries

   Eight rows carry six coefficients and four carry fewer. A coefficient table
   has one width by construction, so four rows have lost entries somewhere
   between the scan and the proposal.

WHY THIS CHECK EARNS ITS PLACE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   A malformed table still COMPILES. LaTeX sets a four-entry row beside a
   six-entry one without complaint and the result looks like a table; nothing
   downstream - not the compile, not the ink metric, not a reader who does not
   already know the width - reports that four coefficients are missing.

   Row-width uniformity is the cheapest structural claim a numeric table makes
   about itself, and it is checkable before anything renders. That ordering is
   the point: rendering an invalid proposal produces a plausible artifact and
   then the question becomes whether the ink agrees, which is a much weaker
   test than counting.

TWO TABLE FORMS, BOTH CHECKED
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   = 1\ 0\ 1\ 0      a numeric run after a relation, space separated
   a & 1 & 2 & 3     array cells, & separated

   EQ0515/0516/0517 use the first, EQ0526 the second. A validator handling only
   one would have passed EQ0526 without looking at it - the failure mode where
   a check reports success because it cannot see the thing.

WHAT IT DOES NOT DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   A proposal with no numeric table passes untouched and reports "no numeric
   table" rather than a vacuous success. Most change requests are not tables,
   and a validator that fired on prose would reject the majority of legitimate
   ones.

   An isolated number in a two-column layout is not a table either: the array
   branch requires at least two numeric cells AND that nearly all cells in the
   row are numeric, so `\text{see} & 2 & \text{below}` is ignored. Without that
   guard the check would reject rows that never claimed uniformity.

   It does not check that the VALUES are right. EQ0515, EQ0516 and EQ0526 pass
   here and that says only that their rows are the same length - whether the
   coefficients match the page is 106's question and a different instrument.

   6 tests.
