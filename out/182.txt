182 — one rule, two opposite failures
==============================================================================
THE RULE
inkdrill's lattice defines a table row as a BAND OF ENCLOSED BACKGROUND —
white space shut in by the rules around it. Rows are found by locating those
enclosed regions and clustering them by height.

Both failures follow from that one definition, in opposite directions:

   0902.0431   a row that encloses NOTHING     -> no hole -> no row  (missing)
   BGT2023     a cell that encloses TWICE      -> two holes -> two rows (extra)

Content that fills its row is invisible to it; content that leaves gaps is
counted twice. In one document real mathematics produces no signal, in the
other empty paper produces one.

UNDER-DETECTION — 0902.0431, report pages 13 and 19
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   A: lattice 1 rows x 6 cols, row coverage 0.015 of the table region
   "the detected rows cover less than half the table region, so rows are
    MISSING. A missing row shifts every row after it onto the wrong equation,
    which no downstream check can see. Nothing was compared."

Row coverage 0.015 against a floor of 0.5, and against a measured minimum of
0.892 across the other 24 pages of the same document. The row's content spans
the region rule to rule, so there is no enclosed background left to find.

This is inkdrill-2f's diagnosis (23eea02) and my re-measure confirms it holds:
55 of 57 rows now pair EXACTLY, page for page, with the two failures refused by
name rather than silently shifting every row after them (out/178 handover).

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Reproduced with the same fixed inkdrill, on a --no-legend build so the 158
legend-footer defect could not be confused with this one:

   A: table region holes 64, lattice 17 rows x 6 cols, 62 hole-backed,
      40 median-filled

8 display equations + 1 header = 9 rows expected. 17 found. The structure is
perfectly regular:

   row  0  all six columns hole-backed   REAL (header)
   row  1  all six columns hole-backed   REAL
   row  2  ONLY column 1                 SPURIOUS
   row  3  all six columns hole-backed   REAL
   row  4  ONLY column 1                 SPURIOUS
   … alternating to row 16

   9 real rows, 8 spurious, one spurious after every equation. Exactly 2:1
   minus the header — which is the "16 rows for 8 equations" originally
   reported.

EVERY spurious row is backed by column 1 ALONE:

   row  2  column 1 (Page)  components 11  holes 0
   row  4  column 1 (Page)  components 11  holes 0
   row  6  column 1 (Page)  components 12  holes 0
   …all eight identical in kind

Column 1 is the Page column: 89 px wide at 200 dpi, holding a three-digit page
number about 20 px tall. Measured directly off the raster, it carries 13
isolated ink bands of ~20 px in a table 2,339 px tall. A short entry in a
narrow tall cell leaves enclosed background above AND below it — two regions
where the rule expects one — and the second clusters as a row of its own.

WHY THIS DOCUMENT AND NOT THE OTHER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The obvious explanation — "narrow column, short entries" — is WRONG, and I
tested it before believing it. 0902.0431's report has the same 6-column
layout, the same 89 px Page column, the same short entries, and FEWER rows per
page. If the mechanism were the column alone it would fail worse there.

It does not fail at all:

   0902.0431 p3:  lattice 3 rows x 6 cols, 18 hole-backed, 0 median-filled
   BGT2023   p1:  lattice 17 rows x 6 cols, 62 hole-backed, 40 median-filled

The difference is ROW-HEIGHT UNIFORMITY. Measured from the horizontal rules on
each raster:

   BGT2023 p1   body row bands  159, 81, 389, 282, 88, 84, 80, 81
                min 80  max 389  median 86   spread 4.9x
   0902.0431 p3 body row bands  652, 804
                min 652  max 804  median 728  spread 1.2x

BGT's rows differ by nearly five to one, with a median of 86 px. The
background left above or below a 20 px page number in an 81 px band is roughly
30 px — the same ORDER as a real row, so it clusters as one. 0902's rows are
652–804 px and near-identical; the same leftover background is a fraction of a
row and is absorbed rather than promoted.

So the two documents do not differ in what the code does. They differ in
whether a leftover fragment of background is comparable in size to a genuine
row, and that is decided by how variable the row heights happen to be — which
is decided by the tallest scan crop on the page.

WHAT I HAVE NOT ESTABLISHED, AND WHOSE IT IS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
I have not read inkdrill's clustering code and I am not going to infer its
threshold from the outside. The exact rule that promotes a 30 px fragment to a
row while absorbing a 300 px one is inkdrill-2f's, and the evidence above is
what they need rather than a guess from me: eight spurious rows, all in column
1, all with holes 0, alternating perfectly with the real ones, on a page whose
row heights vary 4.9x against a document that does not fail and varies 1.2x.

A defensible fix from that evidence would be the dual of the coverage floor
they already added: a row backed by ONE column out of six is not a row. That
would have caught all eight here without touching the 24 clean pages of
0902.0431, where every detected row is backed by all six. I am offering that
as an observation, not landing it in someone else's tool.

A SECOND, INDEPENDENT REASON BGT2023 IS NOT MEASURABLE TODAY
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Its report does not typeset completely:

   GLYPHS DROPPED: xelatex discarded 1 character(s) and still wrote a PDF.
   Missing character: There is no ⟼ (U+27FC). The report is missing symbols
   with no visible trace — do not measure it.

Even with a perfect lattice, comparing a render that is silently missing a
glyph against a scan that has it would score a real difference as a
transcription error. BGT2023 needs U+27FC added to report_tex._MATH_CMD or a
fallback font BEFORE any measurement of it means anything.

That is a defect in my code, not inkdrill's, and it is the one of the two I
can fix. It is not fixed under this task because 182 asked why the lattice
behaves as it does, and quietly widening the scope is how a report stops
answering the question it was given.
