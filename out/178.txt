ALL FOUR GROK PROPOSALS, AND WHY THREE DID NOT PASS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   EQ0515   conf 0.0020   ACCEPTED    ink 91 -> 0    (-91)
   EQ0516   conf 0.0405   rejected    ink 18 -> 23   no improvement
   EQ0517   conf 0.0021   rejected    width uniformity
   EQ0526   conf 1.0      rejected    standalone compile

Every refusal was checked against the source rather than trusted, because the
task's premise is that a refusal of a known-good repair condemns the gate.

EQ0526 — the array declares eight columns and every row supplies nine:

   \begin{array}{llllllll}
   \lambda_0 & 2 & 3 & 4 & 6 & 3 & 2 & 6 & 1 \\      <- 9 cells, 8 declared

   xelatex: "Extra alignment tab has been changed to \cr". This does not
   compile at all. Refusing it is correct.

EQ0517 — twelve rows, of which four carry the wrong number of coefficients:

   row  7: 5     row  8: 5     row 11: 4     row 12: 2      (the other 8: 6)
   row 12:  ...-\tfrac12(\mu_1-\mu_2) &= 0\ 0

   A positive root of e6 has exactly six coordinates (out/164 established the
   page's ground truth: 31 rows, six cells each). Rows of 4 and 2 are
   truncated. Refusing it is correct.

EQ0516 — the only refusal made on MEASUREMENT rather than structure, so it
deserves the most scrutiny:

   scan crop        168 components, 26 holes
   MathPix render   156 components, 20 holes   -> 12 + 6  = 18
   Grok render      178 components, 13 holes   -> 10 + 13 = 23

   Grok's is CLOSER on components and much further on holes. Its proposal
   carries three rows against a region whose scan holds 168 components; the
   lost holes are the digits it does not contain. Rejecting it is defensible
   on the evidence, and it is worth saying plainly that only EQ0515 was ever
   asserted to be a known-good repair — the other three were not, and the loop
   agrees with that in three different ways.

WHAT THIS CONTROL ESTABLISHES, AND WHAT IT DOES NOT
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Establishes: the accept stage takes a good repair, and takes it decisively.
Before this run the loop had 4 acceptances out of 13, all with deltas of
-1, -1, -2 and -18 — small enough that a sceptical reader could have called
them noise. A -91 to an exact match is not noise, and it is the first
demonstration that the instrument can recognise a correct transcription rather
than merely reject bad ones.

Does NOT establish that the loop can PRODUCE such a repair. Grok's EQ0515 was
handed to it. minimax-m3, asked for the same row in the same pipeline,
returned 439 characters for 439 and moved the ink 91 -> 91 (out/174). The
proposer and the verifier are separate problems, and only the verifier has now
been shown to work in both directions.

That distinction matters for what to do next: the gate is not the bottleneck.

ONE THING THE CONTROL EXPOSED ABOUT THE SELECT STAGE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CORRECTION, added later. The paragraph that stood here said EQ0526 "carries
MathPix confidence 1.0 and is genuinely defective — nine cells in an
eight-column array". That is a MISATTRIBUTION and it is wrong.

The nine-cells-in-eight-columns defect belongs to GROK'S PROPOSAL for EQ0526,
which is what this task rejected. MathPix's own reading of EQ0526 passes all
four structural checks — width uniformity, environment balance, CJK, and a
standalone xelatex compile — verified directly. I read a defect off the
proposal and wrote it down as a property of the equation.

What IS true of EQ0526, and is the finding worth keeping:

   MathPix confidence   1.0
   crop ink baseline     38   (scan 21 components / 6 holes,
                               render 56 components / 9 holes)
   structural checks     ALL PASS

Its scan crop carries ink across only x 264..795 of 1244 px while the render
spans 16..1233 — the render puts roughly three times the ink density on the
page that the scan region contains. Something disagrees badly, at a confidence
of 1.0, with nothing structurally wrong to find.

The ink gate WOULD have flagged it: baseline 38 on a row nobody would have
looked at, because `--max-conf 0.5` cannot reach a row MathPix reports no
doubt about. So the selector filters on the one signal that is blind here, and
the signal that is not blind is only consulted afterwards.

That point survives the correction above intact — it never depended on the
equation being structurally malformed, only on it being confidently wrong.

That is a real finding about the design and I am not fixing it under this
task: selecting by measured ink rather than by reported confidence is a
different command with a different cost profile, and it should be decided on
purpose rather than slipped in.
