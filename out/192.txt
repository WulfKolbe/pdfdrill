WHY THE TREE IS CLEAN DESPITE 490 MB OF NEW FILES
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
~/pdfdrill-library is a git repository, and its .gitignore denies by default:

   "This working tree is a 34 GB document library containing copyrighted
    books and personal scanned mail. Nothing is committed unless it is
    explicitly un-ignored below, so an accidental `git add -A` cannot
    publish anything."

The 490 MB the drill produced is invisible to git BY DESIGN. The guard is
working, and a clean `git status` here means "correctly excluded", not
"nothing happened".

TWO INDEPENDENT REASONS NOT TO COMMIT, EITHER SUFFICIENT
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. NONE OF THE FIVE DOCUMENTS IS ELIGIBLE. audit/select_corpus.py states the
   rule, and all three conditions must hold:

      * PDF of at most 1 MB
      * not a Z-Library copy of an in-copyright book
      * not a scan_* folder (personal scanned mail)

   Four of the five are Z-Library books — Mielke, Voloshin, Johnston, Lyche —
   and all five exceed 1 MB, the smallest at 3.4 MB. Committing them would
   publish in-copyright books to a public GitHub repository.

2. THE ARTEFACTS THEMSELVES ARE NEVER COMMITTED, for any document. Even an
   eligible folder contributes only KEEP_SUFFIXES = ('.pdf', '.drill.json').
   The never-commit list names, verbatim, almost everything the drill made:

      *.docmodel.json   *.lines.json   *.inspect.html   **/inspect/
      *.docpack.json    *.tgz          *.zip           **/latex/

   So even for an eligible arXiv paper, inspect.html and the model would stay
   local. The repository is deliberately PDFs plus small JSON.

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE DRILL OUTPUT
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The 490 MB is local-only and that is the intended end state, not a gap. If any
of it is to be shared, the route is the one the ten published documents already
use: copy the specific artefacts into pdfdrill.github.io/reports/<bibkey>/,
where the pages session's allowlist admits exactly report.pdf, report.ink.json,
inspect.html, compare.html and <bibkey>.md — and where a Z-Library book would
be a licensing decision for the user, not a mechanical one for me.

I am not proposing that. Four of these five are in-copyright books.
