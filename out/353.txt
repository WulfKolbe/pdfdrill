353 - three LATW modules, read rather than inferred
===================================================

Source read: ~/MX/LATW/src/latw/{MarginnoteScanner,InlineElementScanner,
ErrorRecoveryModule}.ts. The audit table's TikZ row is why: it framed that
gap as "env list parity (tikzcd, tabularx, longtable)", which named the
wrong thing entirely — 352 found the real gap was the INLINE spellings,
which no entry in an environment list can reach.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. MarginnoteScanner — the one that matters, and pdfdrill has nothing
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT IT DOES. Brace-matches `\marginnote[opts]{...}` and `\sidenote[opts]{...}`
-- a real matcher, not a regex, so nested braces survive. It lifts the body
out as its own unit with kind="marginnote", keeps the full LaTeX including the
optional offset, and leaves `{{key||MARGINNOTE}}` in the flow. The margin note
becomes a first-class object and the body text keeps a pointer to it.

WHAT PDFDRILL HAS. A `Sidenote` DocObject type, produced by
`docmodel/modules/sidenote.py` — from PAGE GEOMETRY. Its own docstring: "a
sidenote in this corpus is a line of type='column' (typically column 0 with
text children)". It reads MathPix's layout, not LaTeX.

The command `\marginnote` appears NOWHERE in src/. Fifty-two hits for
"Sidenote" are all the object type; zero are the construct that produces it.

So the two paths do not overlap: LATW knows a margin note because the AUTHOR
wrote one; pdfdrill knows one because MathPix put a line in column 0. On a
document with source and no MathPix run, pdfdrill has no margin notes at all.

THE THESIS, measured:

    \marginnote                     77
    \sidenote                       30
    of those containing a graphic    24

    extract_graphics over the whole thesis    140 blocks
    of those sitting INSIDE a marginnote       29

The 29 are not lost — that is the part worth stating precisely. They are
found, and they are found as ordinary body figures. A margin figure and a
body figure are indistinguishable in the output, so nothing downstream can
know that `\marginnote[-6cm]{...\begin{tikzpicture}...}` is set in the margin
at a -6cm offset. The gap is the ROLE, not the ink.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. InlineElementScanner — mostly covered, two gaps
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT IT DOES. Four passes, each replacing what it finds with a transclusion:
inline and display math -> a `formula` tiddler ({{key||FO}}); `\includegraphics`
-> an `image` tiddler ({{key||PIC}}), with base64 or SVG variants inlined;
`\ref` -> a `reference` tiddler ({{key||REF}}); `\cite` -> a `citation`
tiddler.

WHAT PDFDRILL HAS, on the source path (build_source_model):

    inline math   COVERED. Formula DocObjects, with content de-duplication,
                  and the code says so: "LATW FormulaScanner / MathPix
                  FormulaProcessor parity".
    citations     COVERED. Citation DocObjects, extract_citations.
    \ref          NOT COVERED. Zero `type="Reference"` in latex_source.py.
                  \ref survives only as text inside a paragraph.
    images        NOT COVERED as an object. extract_graphics returns tikz and
                  tabular blocks; an `\includegraphics` is not a unit of its
                  own on this path, so a figure that is only an included file
                  produces nothing. Zero `type="Picture"`.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. ErrorRecoveryModule — nothing equivalent, and the difference is repair
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT IT DOES. Four repairs before anything is parsed -- mismatched braces,
mismatched `$`, unclosed environments, malformed commands -- and then emits an
`error_report` tiddler carrying the error and warning counts. Damage is fixed
and the fact of the damage is published.

WHAT PDFDRILL HAS. Nothing on the source path, which the audit table already
says. What exists is not the same shape and should not be counted as it:
`compile_fixpoint` DEMOTES a row whose line errors to source-only and
recompiles. That discards the row rather than repairing it, it acts on the
generated report rather than the author's source, and it happens after
LaTeX has already failed once.

The one thing pdfdrill does better here is the reporting half: a demoted row
is counted and named in the build output, so the damage is visible. What is
missing is the repair.
