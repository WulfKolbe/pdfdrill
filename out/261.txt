261 — the abstract/TOC swap: both labels are read, and one object claims 99%
      of the document
==============================================================================

DOCUMENT: idea14-proceedings   12,001 lines, 219 TOC lines, 19 abstract lines
(the clearest of the 46 corpus documents carrying both types)

ARE THE TYPES READ, OR IS THE LABEL INFERRED FROM TEXT? READ.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Neither is guessed. `AbstractProcessor.find_items` gates on
`payload["type"] == "abstract"` and nothing else. `TocProcessor.find_items`
gates on membership of the four `table_of_contents_*` types. The three regexes
in abstract.py (`\begin{abstract}`, `\end{abstract}`, `\section*{Abstract}`)
run only on text that the TYPE already selected, and they strip wrappers — they
never decide what an abstract is.

So this is not a detection failure. Both labels arrive correct and both are
believed. The break is in the association afterwards.

BREAK 1 — ONE Toc OBJECT, REALIZED OVER THE WHOLE BOOK
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
`TocProcessor` collects every `table_of_contents_*` line in the document into a
SINGLE Toc and realizes it as one contiguous range:

    Realization(start=anchors[0], end=anchors[-1])

This document has TOC lines on page 6 (the front table of contents) and again
on page 130 (back matter). The first and last are therefore 11,898 anchors
apart, and the Toc's realization is:

    anchors 101 .. 11,999   =  11,899 of 12,001 lines   =  99% of the document

What is inside that claimed range:

    text                    10,271
    section_header             280
    column                     231
    simple_cell                129
    page_info                  125
    figure_label               107
    diagram                     92
    table_of_contents_item      88   <- the 219 lines it is actually about
    equation_number             84

and 18 of the 19 abstract lines. Resolving "which object realizes this line"
returns Toc for essentially every line in the book, and for 18 abstracts it
returns `Abstract+Page+Toc`. That is the swap: not a mislabelled abstract, but
a Toc whose range swallows them.

The range is a claim about which anchors the object IS. A Toc that is 219 lines
claiming 11,899 is false regardless of who reads it. Today nothing walks it —
`slice_anchors` over a surface range is used by the dehyphenators,
promote_cleaned and tiddlywiki's paragraph rebuild, all Paragraph-scoped, and
`DocumentFlowProcessor` keys only on `surface[0]`, so the reading order is
unaffected. The falsehood is inherited by the next consumer, not by a current
one, and I am not going to report a symptom I did not observe.

BREAK 2 — 65 ENTRIES RECORDED 215 TIMES
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The four TOC types are a containment hierarchy: container -> rows -> (item,
number). `find_items` walks all four at the same level, appending one entry per
LINE, so every entry is recorded once per level it appears at:

    table_of_contents_row       65   the joined text of its children
    table_of_contents_item      88   the same words again, on their own
    table_of_contents_number    62   the same page number again, on its own
    table_of_contents_container  4   contributes NOTHING (its children are
                                     rows, whose own text is empty — the
                                     content sits one level further down, so
                                     the join resolves to "")
    ----
                               215   entries recorded, 170 distinct

The real entry count is 65. The repeats are visible in the data:

    entries[0:6] = ['Invited Talks', 'Invited Talks',
                    'Information Visualization for Knowledg',
                    'Information Visualization for Knowledg',
                    'Ben Shneiderman', '..... 8']

'Invited Talks' twice — once as the row, once as the item. And '..... 8' is
recorded as an ENTRY in its own right: a bare leader-dots-and-page-number with
no title attached. '..... 10', '..... 19', '..... 26' and '..... 67' each
appear five times.

WHAT IS NOT BROKEN
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The abstract side is sound and should not be touched:

    19 abstract lines -> 19 Abstract objects, every one non-empty
       p5  2,662 chars   'Preface \n\nData, data everywhere; massive datasets…'
       p8    824 chars   'Interactive information visualization tools provide…'
       p9  1,322 chars   'This talk will consist of two parts…'
       p10   674 chars   'In this demo paper we introduce VizLinc…'

    parents: 16 under a `column` line, 3 with none.

The abstract LINE's own `text` is empty in all 19 cases — the content is in its
children (10 to 30 each), which `find_items` already joins. That is the shape,
not a defect.

A LESSER FINDING, RECORDED WHILE HERE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
abstract.py's docstring says "Multiple abstract lines collapse into one
DocObject when they appear contiguously." The code does no such thing — it
emits one Abstract per line unconditionally. For a proceedings volume that is
the RIGHT behaviour (19 papers, 19 abstracts) and the docstring is simply
describing something that was never written. Not fixed here; 261 is a
diagnosis.

THE DIAGNOSIS IN ONE LINE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Both types are read correctly. One Toc object is emitted per DOCUMENT rather
than per table of contents, its realization spans first-TOC-line to
last-TOC-line and therefore contains the abstracts, and its entries are the
same 65 rows counted once per level of a four-level containment hierarchy.

Nothing changed in this task.
