FOUND WHILE PUBLISHING 676. Four of the twenty published
`evidence-formula.pdf` files came out OVER the 20 MB budget that
`reports/budget.py` exists to hold them under:

    document                          predicted   actual   over by
    0902.0431                          17.5 MB   20.6 MB    +18%
    johnston-linear-matrix-algebra     18.4 MB   21.8 MB    +18%
    lyche-numerical-linear-algebra     17.9 MB   20.7 MB    +16%
    cardona-qft-methods                    --    22.3 MB      --

(cardona's budget line was not captured in the retained stdout tail; its
actual is the largest of the four.)

THE MECHANISM. `budget.choose_rung` walks the measured ladder (scale/quality
-> fraction of original: 1.00/q75 = 62%, 0.85/q75 = 48%, 0.70/q75 = 37%,
0.60/q72 = 29%, 0.50/q70 = 22%, 0.42/q70 = 17%) and picks the first rung
whose PREDICTED size fits the budget. On these four it picked a rung
predicting 17.5-18.4 MB against a 20 MB budget -- a 9-14% margin -- and the
compile landed 16-18% above the prediction, which eats the margin and
crosses the line.

So the ladder is not wrong; the prediction is optimistic, and the margin it
leaves is smaller than its own error. A budget that is satisfied by a
prediction rather than by the artefact is not a budget, it is an estimate
with a name -- the same shape as rule 22, one level down: the check
correctly answered "does the predicted size fit" and never asked "does the
file".

NOT A BLOCKER, BY THE USER'S OWN TOLERANCE: "no size problem, I could even
open 30MB file in 1-2 seconds from github.io". All four are between 20.6 and
22.3 MB. Published as they are.

THE FIX IS NOT A BIGGER MARGIN. Two options, both cheap:

  (a) MEASURE, THEN DECIDE. Compile at the chosen rung, check the real
      file, and step down one rung and recompile if it missed. Costs one
      extra xelatex run on the documents that miss (4 of 20 here).
  (b) CALIBRATE THE PREDICTOR against the corpus it now has. 20 documents x
      4 kinds of measured (predicted, actual) pairs exist in
      `~/pdfdrill-library/out/republish_676.json` today; the residual is
      what the predictor is missing, and a per-kind correction factor from
      that population is a one-line change with a named population behind
      it (rule 2).

(b) first, because it costs nothing at build time and the data is already on
disk; (a) as the backstop, since a calibrated predictor is still a
prediction. `commands._status_budget_notes` already audits over-budget
published files, so the audit exists -- it just has nothing upstream
honouring it.

NOT DONE. Recorded with its measurement; the publish did not wait on it.
