288 + 289 — the extractor bugs, and the field that made them almost irrelevant
==============================================================================

288 — THE THREE REGRESSING DOCUMENTS, NAMED
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
All three fail in the extracted preamble, and all three name a different bug.

  2208.01506   ! LaTeX Error: Missing \begin{document}.
               The extractor emits, at line 23:
                   \providecommand{\appendix@section}{}\renewcommand\appendix@section[1]{
               As written in NotesDAG.tex:
                   \newcommand\appendix@section[1]{%
               `@` is a letter only between \makeatletter and \makeatother.
               Lifted out bare, `\appendix@section` parses as `\appendix`
               followed by the text `@section`, the brace count goes wrong, and
               LaTeX reports a missing \begin{document} a hundred lines later —
               naming neither the macro nor the line that caused it.

               (A second, separate cause appears when TEXINPUTS is not set:
               \usepackage{articlemacro} is a LOCAL .sty in the e-print. It is
               there, and the render path already passes TEXINPUTS, so this one
               is not a bug — it was my probe that was wrong.)

  2602.23211   ! Illegal parameter number in definition of \hgnodea.
               Extracted:      \def\hgnodea{#1}          (top level, alone)
               As written, tikz-hypergraph.tex:121:
                   \pgfextra{
                     \def\hgnodea{#1}
                     \def\hgnodeb{#2}
                   }
               nested inside a \tikzset body. `#1` is legal only inside a
               definition that has a parameter. `_collect_macro_defs` already
               had a nesting guard, but it tested "inside a span I previously
               captured" — and \tikzset blocks are collected separately, so
               nothing covered this one.

  2508.08293v1 ! Undefined control sequence at
                   \providecommand{\colim}{}\renewcommand\colim{\textup{colim}}
               the same robustification shape as 2208.01506.

FIXED IN THE EXTRACTOR, NOT THE FALLBACK
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  latex_source._collect_macro_defs
      nesting is now BRACE DEPTH, not previously-captured spans. A \def at
      depth > 0 belongs to the body that encloses it.
  latex_source.standalone_preamble
      definitions are wrapped in \makeatletter/\makeatother when any name
      contains `@`. Only the definitions — a \usepackage inside \makeatletter
      is legal but changes catcodes during the package's own loading, which is
      not something to do on spec.
  latex_source._DEF_START
      + \newlength, + \newsavebox (287). 2512.15098v4 defines \ImageMaxHeight
      as a \newlength and used it in every one of its 21 regions.
  commands._render_regions
      RE-EXTRACTS from meta.latex_preamble.original rather than trusting the
      stored `standalone`, which was computed when injectlatex ran. Otherwise
      an extractor fix reaches only documents re-injected afterwards.

AND IT DID NOT HELP THESE THREE. Each still fails 0/6 with the author's
preamble. The three constructs above are real and now handled; what remains is
something else in preambles of 3,000-4,600 characters, and I did not chase it
further because 289 showed the ground had moved.

289 — THE FIELDS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
    207 regions across the 10 sampled documents

    carry `latex_original`                      176
      whose value DIFFERS from latex_code        40
    `macros_unresolved` non-empty                 0
    field the renderer compiles           latex_code   (`latex or latex_code`)

And latex_code is the MACRO-EXPANDED form. The pair, from 2002.06055:

    latex_code      \begin{tikzcd} \mathsf{C}(Y,Z) \times \mathsf{C}(X,Y) …
    latex_original  \begin{tikzcd} \C(Y,Z) \times \C(X,Y) …

So the renderer was already compiling the field with no author macros in it,
and `macros_unresolved` is empty on all 207 — which says the same thing from
the other side. I had been injecting a preamble to resolve macros that were
already resolved.

THE PAIRING, MEASURED
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
    A  expanded field + GENERIC preamble      151/207   (72.9%)
    B  original field + AUTHOR preamble        70/176
    -- 286's fallback (expanded, author then generic)  165/207 (79.7%)

289's hypothesis is half right, and the half that is right matters. Macro
resolution is NOT on the critical path: A at 72.9% uses no author preamble at
all, and the gain over the 138/207 (66.7%) that generic-only scored in 286 is
+13 from `\graphicspath` and TEXINPUTS — the author's FIGURE FILES — not from
the preamble.

The other half: the author's preamble is still worth ~14 rows (165 against
151), and it is not macros. I tested what else it could be by building a
STYLES-ONLY preamble — the generic head plus the author's \usetikzlibrary,
\definecolor and \tikzset blocks and nothing else:

    2004.05631   styles-only 16/25   generic 16/25   full author 21/25
    2210.06079   styles-only 16/25   generic 16/25   full author 25/25
    2002.06055   styles-only 23/25   generic 23/25   full author 23/25

Styles-only is identical to generic. So the remaining value is in the author's
PACKAGES, not their macros and not their styles — which is a narrower and more
tractable question than "inject the preamble", and it is where I would look
next.

WHAT I DID NOT ANSWER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
288 also asked how many regions compile under the generic preamble while still
referencing an author macro — compiling wrongly rather than correctly. On this
corpus the answer is bounded by construction: `macros_unresolved` is empty on
all 207 and the compiled field is the expanded one, so a region referencing an
undefined author macro would fail rather than compile. The 40 regions whose
`latex_original` differs are precisely the ones where expansion did something,
and it is the EXPANDED form that is compiled. I did not verify the rendered
output pixel-wise against the scan, which is the only way to catch a macro that
expanded to something wrong rather than something missing — that is 281.
