  THAT ESTIMATE IS A FLOOR, NOT A PREDICTION. 295's rate is the rate of
  `reporttex`, which builds a LaTeX report. The ink pass runs inkdrill over
  the rendered report pages, which is different work. The one inkdrill
  timing measured this session - reportpages page detection on a 16-page
  report - took 127 s, about seven times 295's per-document rate. At that
  cost the 267 would be 9.4 h rather than 1.4 h. The honest range is
  1.4 to 9.4 hours and the spread is because inkdrill's own per-document
  cost on this work has never been measured. Measuring it on ten documents
  would cost about twenty minutes and would replace the range with a
  number.

A LIMIT OF THE FILTER ITSELF, measured not assumed

  Formula objects carry confidence: None. Not a low value - no value. In
  0707.4470 that is 213 Formulas against 62 Equations, and corpus-wide no
  Formula anywhere falls below 0.5 because none of them can.

  So 'documents with a low-confidence equation' selects on the minority
  object type. If the point of the ink pass is to find OCR that went wrong,
  this filter is blind to the majority of the mathematics by construction.
  Reporting '0 formulas below 0.5' as a quality result would have been the
  same error as reading a type name for a measurement.

  The 369 is the answer to the question asked. Whether it is the right
  scope depends on whether formulas are meant to be in it, and that is a
  decision I have not taken.

  Also: 431 of the 1,349 documents carry no confidence on any object, so
  they cannot enter or fail this filter - they are simply not selectable
  by it.
