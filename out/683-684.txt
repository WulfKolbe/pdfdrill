and BOTH `residuals --measure` and `marks --build` invoke inkdrill as a
subprocess. Two writers on one tool's working directories is the 2026-09-11

THE INK DID NOT MOVE, AND COULD NOT
------------------------------------
Distribution byte-identical to the previous measurement: clean 158,
component 56, noise 698, stable 21, unrendered 2, weak 121.

`prefer_refined: False`. The measure build reads raw `latex`, so it still
measures MathPix's `\downharpoonleft` readings while the evidence PDFs read
the repairs. The re-measure did the ONE thing it was needed for — re-stamping
against the current model so the timestamp gate could pass.

This is the third time this question has come up and it is still the user's to
settle: the ink is a measurement OF MATHPIX'S OUTPUT. Making it read our own
corrections would improve the corpus's numbers without MathPix having
improved, which is the one thing a quality measurement must not do. The
counter-argument is that "correct residuals" ought to describe what a reader
actually sees. `--prefer-refined` exists on the measure path either way.

not a problem to be worked around — which is why 684 exists rather than a
