BUT THE SPLIT IS THE LEAST INTERESTING RESULT HERE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   These 9 rows are a CONTROL GROUP and I did not appreciate that until the
   numbers were in front of me. Their SLT distance is zero by selection, so
   MathPix, the author and (barring a revised paper) the printed page all
   carry the SAME expression. Whatever ink distance remains cannot be a
   content difference; it is the measurement's own noise for comparing a
   LaTeX RENDER against a SCAN.

   Those 9 distances are: [2, 5, 9, 10, 11, 12, 12, 17, 20]
   median 11, max 20.

   So render-vs-scan noise on identical content runs to 20 here, not 6.
   The floor of 6 is the p95 of the same render through two RASTERIZERS -
   it bounds ghostscript-vs-poppler, and nothing else. Comparing a Computer
   Modern render against a scan of a journal's typesetting adds a different
   typeface, different hinting and scan noise on top of that, none of which
   the rasterizer control can see. Applied to render-vs-scan, a floor of 6
   is roughly three times too tight, and 7 of these 9 'persists' verdicts
   are most likely the floor being wrong rather than the page differing.

WHAT I COULD NOT REPRODUCE, STATED AS AN OPEN QUESTION
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   Three rows fell enormously: EQ0057 85 -> 2, EQ0016 100 -> 12,
   EQ0010 84 -> 12. The same content measured from the REPORT CELL is
   wildly further from the scan than the same content measured standalone.
   I tried to reproduce the large values from the content and could not:
      shrink-to-fit  (\FitMath at 75% / 55% / 40%) -> d = 7, 4, 5
      resolution     (60 - 278 px tall)            -> d = 1 - 16
   component count stayed 63 throughout both sweeps. So the large
   report-cell distances are not a property of the LaTeX at any scale or
   resolution I can produce, which points at how the cell is isolated from
   the report page rather than at what it contains. That is a question for
   the ink side, offered as evidence rather than as a diagnosis of code I
   did not write.

ADDENDUM - THE RENDER-VS-SCAN FLOOR, MEASURED UNCONDITIONED
====================================================================================

   The nine rows above were selected on ink distance > 6, so they are a
   TRUNCATED TAIL, not a distribution. The ink side made that point and it
   is correct: my 'median 11' was an over-estimate by exactly the same
   mechanism I criticised in out/040 (a sample conditioned on a correlate of
   the outcome). Re-measured properly, over EVERY SLT-zero equation in the
   20 documents that carry author LaTeX, unconditioned on ink distance:

   n = 808   (810 SLT-zero rows; 2 demoted, excluded and labelled)
   Content is identical by construction - MathPix's LaTeX equals the
   author's structurally - so every one of these distances is measurement,
   not disagreement.

      min 0   p50 2   p75 5   p90 12   p95 22   p99 39   max 75   mean 4.6

      above  6 :  158  (19.6%)
      above 11 :   92  (11.4%)
      above 20 :   45  ( 5.6%)

   BOTH earlier numbers were wrong, in opposite directions:
      a floor of 6 falsely flags 19.6% of content-identical rows - about
      one in five, which is a large false-positive rate for the class the
      findings are ranked on;
      my 'median 11' was three times the true median of 2. The tail is long
      (p99 = 39, max 75) but the body is tight.

   p95 = 22 is the defensible render-vs-scan floor on this evidence.
   It sits close to the max of my truncated sample, which is coincidence
   rather than corroboration - the sample could not have produced it.
