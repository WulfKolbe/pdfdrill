263 — the dot leader comes off in the model, once
==============================================================================

WHAT THE CORPUS CARRIES
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
    entry strings from table_of_contents_* lines      54,240
      carrying a dot leader                           33,946   (62.6%)
        ...that are ONLY a leader and a number        16,870
        ...that split into title + locator            16,845
        ...where the dots are not a title/number
           boundary ("..... a", "..... page")            231
      with no leader at all                           20,294
    documents affected                                   153

The only leader character MathPix emits in this corpus is `.` — checked across
all 54,240 entries, and there is no `·`, `…`, `_` or dash leader anywhere. So
the rule is one character class, not a guess at typography.

WHY STRIPPING THE LEADER ALONE WOULD HAVE BEEN WORSE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
`table_of_contents_number` is its own type, so on a NUMBER line the parts are
already separate. But a ROW's entry is the join of its children, and there the
three parts arrive fused:

    "1.1. Cayley algebra \(\mathfrak{C}\)  ..... 1"

Deleting the dots gives "1.1. Cayley algebra \(\mathfrak{C}\) 1" — the page
number welded to the title, which is worse than leaving the dots in. The
locator has to be lifted out in the same operation, which is what
`split_leader` does.

948 rows made that concrete. A proceedings TOC row reads

    "Discrete Complex Analysis on Planar Quad-Graphs  ..... 57 \n
     Alexander I. Bobenko and Felix Günther"

The authors belong to the entry; the page number does not belong to the title.
The locator is therefore taken as what sits between the leader and the first
line break, and anything past that break stays with the title. A first version
of the rule required the whole tail to be a page number, so all 948 of these
fell to the fallback and got "… Quad-Graphs 57 Alexander I. Bobenko" — the
exact welding the rule exists to prevent. Found by printing the cases the rule
did NOT classify, rather than counting the ones it did.

A second sub-case in the same 948: 231 entries whose locator is a dotted
section number (`3.1.2.1`, `4.6.2`) rather than a page — an index pointing at
sections. The locator pattern accepts arabic, roman, dotted, ranged and
letter-suffixed forms.

WHAT CHANGED
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  src/docmodel/modules/toc.py
    `split_leader(text) -> (title, locator)`, applied once in `find_items`.

    `entries`   now leader-stripped TITLES. The 16,870 leader-only lines
                contribute nothing, because they have no title by construction
                — they are the `table_of_contents_number` lines, and "..... 7"
                was being recorded as an entry in its own right (261, break 2).
    `rows`      new: [{title, locator, raw, line_type}] per contributing line,
                so a consumer never parses dots out of a string again and the
                page number is not lost by the stripping.

  src/docops/projectors/plaintext.py
    Renders from `rows` — "title  locator" — so the faithful page dump keeps
    the page numbers it always showed, without the leader.

VERIFICATION
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Replayed over all 54,240 corpus entries with the shipped function:

    leaders surviving into a title                           0
    entries reduced to a title + locator                16,845
    entries correctly reduced to no title              16,870
    entries with dots removed but no locator found         231

12 tests, including every locator form, the multi-line row, and an assertion
that no title anywhere contains "..".

ONE MISTAKE WORTH RECORDING
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
I first wrote `entry_strings.append(title or raw)`. The `or raw` was meant to
protect entries with no leader — which do not need it, since their title IS the
raw text. What it actually did was put every leader-only line back into
`entries` as "..... 7", re-admitting the precise junk this task removes. Caught
by a test that asserted the entry list, not by the corpus replay, which counted
titles and never looked at what `entries` ended up holding.
