382/383 - the pairs path, and what the table path was costing
=============================================================

THROUGHPUT

    compare-pairs    97 pairs in 11.2 s   =  521 pairs/min
    the table path   ~6 rows/min, ~60 s per page
                     + 487 s lattice detection for 34 pages (14.3 s/page)
                       before a single cell is compared

  87x. None of the removed work was measurement. `compare` renders a PDF
  page, finds the lattice, locates two cells per row and measures those —
  correct when the two images exist only AS a printed table, pure transport
  when the caller already holds both as files, which a generator always does
  because it rendered them.

  The measurement is byte-identical: mathstruct.pair_stats, the same
  definition `compare` uses, and the same thresholds. A pair row and a table
  row cannot be classified by two drifting rules.

THE MANIFEST (383)

  ~/pdfdrill-library/datikz-fixture/pairs.json, written by the report
  builder at the moment the renders are made and their identifiers are still
  in hand. 97 pairs; 3 of the 100 rows have no render and are absent rather
  than paired with a blank.

  It carries what the reader of a distance needs: which side is which, what
  the comparison measures, the split, and the caveat below.

DISTRIBUTION over the 97

    class      clean 21 (22%)   noise 23 (24%)   weak 20 (21%)   component 33 (34%)
    distance   min 0   p25 2   median 7   p75 30   p90 94   max 206
               identical five-tuples: 21
               at or below the noise floor of 7: 49 (51%)

  READ THAT AS A CEILING ON PRECISION, NOT AS THE FLOOR 281 ASKED FOR.

  It is not scale-controlled. Our renders are a MEDIAN 0.3x the dataset's
  pixel area — the dataset fixes every image at 448x448, we render at the
  figure's natural size — and holes resolve with resolution. The tail shows
  it plainly: DTZ00036 has 41 components on both sides and 8 holes against
  212; DTZ00039, 20 against 133; DTZ00037, 23 against 142. Those are not
  rendering disagreements, they are the same drawing rasterised at different
  sizes.

  It is not uniform, which is why it took measuring rather than assuming:
  the median holes ratio is 1.00 and only 27 of 90 pairs have more holes on
  the dataset's side. The scale difference dominates the TAIL, not the body.

  So 51% at or below the noise floor is a real result — half these figures
  render structurally identically across two LaTeX installations — and the
  p90 of 94 is mostly an artefact of comparing a 315x290 render against a
  448x448 one. The honest floor needs both sides rasterised at one size, and
  that is the next measurement rather than this one.

  340's rule is what should have caught this before the run: a size
  difference between the things being compared puts a scale difference into
  the residual. It was written about column widths and it applies to
  rasters.
