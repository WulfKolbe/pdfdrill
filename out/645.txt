THE FINDING — WHERE A CITATION'S SPAN COMES FROM, AND WHAT IS WRONG WITH IT
  The brief suspected the three `bibliography.py` detectors record no span at
  creation. They DO — all three write `offset`/`length` on a `surface`
  Realization, and on penev_A **0 of 81 citations lacked a span**, before or
  after. The defect is a different one and it is the reason citations did not
  reach the prose:

  A CITATION GROUP IS N OBJECTS SHARING ONE SPAN.
    `detect_numeric_citations` (`[1,2,3]`), `detect_author_year_citations`
    (`(Oja 1989; Sanger 1989)`) and `detect_author_year_in_objects` each
    create one Citation per key and give EVERY one of them the span of the
    WHOLE GROUP. The projector then substituted them one at a time, and
    `_apply_line_substitutions` (correctly, defensively) drops a substitution
    that overlaps one already accepted — so one key won and the rest were
    dropped on the floor. penev_A: 11 spans hold 25 citations; 14 citations
    were lost that way and with them 12 of 52 REF tiddlers' only inbound
    link. `(Linsker 1988; Oja 1989; Sanger 1989; Földiák 1990; Plumbey 1991)`
    is one span at offset 1, length 65 carrying four Citations; one survived.
    This is 646-c, measured and now fixed.

  AND `CitationProcessor` (model build, the `[key]` scan) does the opposite:
    `_split_offsets` gives each key in `[a, b]` its OWN sub-span. Nothing was
    dropped there — but each key was replaced separately INSIDE the source
    brackets, and the `CIT` template renders `[<$link…>key</$link>]`, so the
    reader got `[[a], [b]]`.

  Both shapes are the same fact: a citation group is ONE piece of prose, so
  it is ONE substitution.

  AND ONE SPAN WAS BEING GUESSED. `detect_author_year_in_objects` reads an
  OBJECT's joined text and recorded `m.start()` — a position in THAT text —
  against the object's own Realization, whose `start` is the object's FIRST
  line. On any paragraph longer than one line that offset names a character
  position that does not exist on that line: the projector substitutes the
  wrong stretch of prose, or (once the offset runs past the line) nothing.
  It is now LOOKED UP: `_span_on_its_own_line` walks the lines the
  realization spans and returns the group's real position on the line it is
  actually on. When no line reproduces the group (a mutator rewrote the
  object's text) NO span is recorded — the citation is still anchored, so it
  still gets its Reference stub and its `cites` edge, and `spans_unrecorded`
  counts it. A position is never invented (rule 5).

      them to the new `_citation_groups`, which merges spans that are

      `;` — the three shapes a multi-key citation takes — and yields ONE
      substitution per group holding one `{{<REF title>||CIT}}` per distinct
      key in source order. A finished group flanked by a matching `[...]` or

WHAT IS STILL ORPHANED, AND WHY — 645-a
  4 of 52 penev_A REF tiddlers and 5 of 23 penev_B ones have no inbound link
  after the fix. Every one of them was checked, not assumed: the citation's
  line is owned by a FOOTNOTE, and a Footnote tiddler's body is copied from
  `props["content"]` VERBATIM. Inline substitution (`FO`, `CIT`, `FN`, the
  picture URLs) runs on PARAGRAPH bodies only — `_transclude_paragraph` is
  the one place `subs_by_line` is applied. So no formula and no citation
  inside a footnote, sidenote, list item or abstract has EVER become a
  transclusion, on any document. That is a bigger class than these 9 links
  (it is the same mechanism behind the 36 penev_A / 20 penev_B unreachable
  Footnotes, 646-a2) and it is named rather than half-fixed here: the
  footnote body is a JOINED string and the offsets are per-line, so wiring it
  up is its own measurement.

A SECOND FINDING, NAMED NOT FIXED — 645-b
  `detect_author_year_citations` (the mathpix-lines detector, which produced
  80 of penev_A's 81 citations) matches a surname with
  `\b([A-Z][A-Za-z\-']{1,})` — ASCII only. `detect_author_year_in_objects`,
  the same job on the object path, uses `_AY_SURNAME`
  (`[A-ZÀ-Þ][A-Za-zÀ-ſ\-']{1,}`) plus `_fold_key`. So the group
  `(Linsker 1988; Oja 1989; Sanger 1989; Földiák 1990; Plumbey 1991)`
  produces FOUR citations on penev_A and not five: `Földiák` matches
  nothing and the reference is silently absent from the model. Confirmed by
  running both patterns against the string. Not fixed here — a surname class
  change alters citekeys corpus-wide and deserves its own measurement, not a
  line inside a task about transclusion.
