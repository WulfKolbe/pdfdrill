638 — the footnote markers the LaTeX projector left as inline maths
==================================================================

THE USER'S REPORT, CHECKED FIRST RATHER THAN ASSUMED
  "penev_A.tex still holds 94 bare ^{n} in running text, 0 \footnotemark,
   0 \footnote{"                                        SUBSTANTIALLY TRUE.
    On this machine at HEAD (29185c8) the .tex held 0 \footnotemark and 0
    \footnote{, and 146 bare `\({ }^{n}\)` inline-maths spans, of which 104
    come from Paragraph text (the running text the user counted) and 42 from
    inside Footnote bodies. The user's 94 is the same class measured in
    another environment. There are 220 `{ }^{n}` occurrences in all and 487
    `^{n}`; the other 74 and 267 are REAL exponents (`\sigma_{r}{ }^{2}`,
    `10^{12}`) and must not be touched: a `{ }^{n}` is a marker only when it
    is the WHOLE inline-math span, which is what `marker_refnum` decides.

  "the mapping is already in the model: 53 Footnote objects each carrying
   refnum and anchor_marker"                             TRUE, with a caveat
    that turns out to be the whole difficulty: `anchor_marker` is not a
    LOOKUP. `FootnoteProcessor.create_object` builds it by string
    concatenation from `refnum` (`"{ }^{" + refnum + "}"`), and `refnum`
    itself is read off the BODY line, not off the marker. So the marker's
    own position — page, line, offset — is recorded NOWHERE in the model.

  "refnum is not unique"                                 TRUE and measured:
    16 refnums name more than one Footnote on penev_A (9 six times, 3 five
    times, 2/1/21/24 four times each), 12 on penev_B.

  "the Formula module CLAIMS `{ }^{n}` as a Formula today" (the brief's
    premise for 640)                                     NOT TRUE at HEAD.
    `FormulaProcessor.find_items` already skips a body matching
    `_is_footnote_marker`, and penev_A carries 0 Formula objects whose latex
    is a bare superscript marker (674 Formulas, 0 of them a marker). The
    markers reach the .tex through the PARAGRAPH'S OWN TEXT, verbatim. 640's
    "move the claim upstream" therefore has nothing to move on these two
    documents; what it can still do is give the marker an OBJECT.

WHERE THE MARKER'S POSITION CAME FROM — NOT GUESSED, LOOKED UP
  The model does not record it, and this task did not invent one. It is
  recoverable EXACTLY: the marker is in the paragraph's own `mathpix_lines`
  lines, and every line carries `_page`. `footnotes._marker_pages` walks the
  lines the object is anchored at, finds the markers per line, and accepts
  the page only when the refnum SEQUENCE off the lines equals the refnum
  sequence in the object's materialised text — an exact alignment, not a
  best guess. It holds on 63 of 63 penev_A paragraphs and every penev_B one:
  `markers_without_a_page` is 0 on both. When it does not hold the object's
  own page is used only if every line it covers is on ONE page; failing
  that the marker is unresolved and counted (rule 5).

THE DISAMBIGUATION, IN ONE LINE
  A marker `{ }^{n}` on page P takes the Footnote with refnum n whose body
  page is P (marker order on the page against body order on the page when
  there is more than one, and every such pairing is counted AMBIGUOUS);
  failing that, the nearest LATER page within one page; failing that the
  marker is left byte-for-byte as it stands and counted.

  THE ONE-PAGE CAP IS A DELIBERATE NARROWING OF THE BRIEF, AND IT COST 10
  RESOLUTIONS. The brief says "the nearest LATER page (a body can spill to
  the next page)". Implemented as written — nearest later page, unbounded —
  penev_A resolved 31 of 104 markers. MEASURED, only ONE of those eleven
  forward matches was a genuine +1 spill; the other ten sat 4, 4, 4, 5, 6,
  6, 7, 8, 11 and 12 pages away. A body twelve pages later is not that
  marker's footnote, it is the next chapter's footnote n — which is content
  MIXED UP, the other half of the conservation claim 646 exists to measure.
  `MAX_SPILL_PAGES = 1`; penev_A resolves 21 instead of 31 and the ten wrong
  attachments are counted as unresolved instead of printed as fact.

  A BODY IS NEVER SEARCHED BACKWARD. 23 penev_A markers have bodies only on
  EARLIER pages; resolving backward would attach the previous chapter's n.

THE CONVENTION FOR THE OUTPUT — ONE, STATED
  `\footnotemark[n]` at the marker, `\footnotetext[n]{…}` at the END of the
  paragraph that carries it. NOT `\footnote{…}` at the marker: `\footnote`
  renumbers from LaTeX's own counter, and the whole point of `refnum` is the
  number the publication printed. `\footnotemark[n]`/`\footnotetext[n]` keeps
  it. A body no marker took is still emitted where it stands, but now as
  `\footnotetext[n]{…}` — a bare `\footnotetext{…}` never steps the counter,
  so all 54 penev_A / 41 penev_B of them printed as footnote "0".

THE TWO COMPILE ERRORS THAT WENT AWAY, AND THE ONE FATAL ONE
  `_escape_text` escaped `_` across the WHOLE string, math included, so a
  footnote body carrying `\(F_{r}^{same}\)` was emitted as `\(F\_{r}…\)`.
  Both of penev_A's "! Missing $ inserted." errors were that, on the two
  copies of one duplicated body. Bodies now go through `_prose`, whose
  `escape_prose_specials` is math-aware. penev_A: 2 errors -> 0.

  `_balance_braces` — the brace analogue of the projector's existing
  `balance_math`, applied per prose block. MEASURED, not defensive: 1
  penev_A and 4 penev_B Paragraphs carry a `\footnotetext{` MathPix left in
  the prose of a line the paragraph ALSO claims (646's Footnote+Paragraph
  doubly-claimed class), and ONE of penev_B's is never closed. Emitted
  verbatim it swallowed the rest of the file — "File ended while scanning
  use of \@footnotetext", Emergency stop — and penev_B's projection had
  never compiled past page 22 on any run, before this task or after it.
  0 Footnote BODIES are unbalanced on either document; the runaway is in
  prose, which is where the containment now sits.

WHAT IS DELIBERATELY LEFT ALONE, AND WHY
  A marker inside a FOOTNOTE body (42 penev_A / 31 penev_B) is NOT a
  reference. `FootnoteProcessor._parse_footnotetext` keeps only the first
  `\footnotetext{…}` on a line, and MathPix routinely puts two footnotes on
  one line, so 29 of penev_A's 53 bodies end with the NEXT footnote's
  printed label and its text merged in ("…(Deco and Obradovic 1996).
  \({ }^{4}\) PCA has also been utilized…"). Emitting `\footnotemark[4]`
  there would assert a reference that is not there. Left, counted, named as
  638-b.
  A marker inside a SIDENOTE body (93 / 39) never reaches the .tex at all —
  `LaTeXProjector._render` has no Sidenote branch. Named as 638-c.
  The TiddlyWiki projector's own `_substitute_footnotes` is UNCHANGED. It
  still resolves through the document-wide `fn_by_refnum` (first footnote
  with that printed number wins), which is 644-a / 646-a. Moving it onto
  this resolver would move every number 645 and 646 published; it is 638-d.

CONCERNS
  21 of 104 and 15 of 55 is a low resolution rate and it is not smoothed
  over: 79 of the 123 unresolved markers on the two documents have no
  Footnote object to resolve TO, which no rule can fix. The rate the
  disambiguation itself is responsible for is 21 of 25 (penev_A) and 15 of
  18 (penev_B) among markers that HAVE a candidate body on their own page.
  16 penev_A and 9 penev_B pairings are AMBIGUOUS — two bodies with one
  refnum on one page, paired by order. Order on the page is the best
  available evidence and it is what a compositor used, but it is evidence,
  not proof, and it is counted separately so a reader can discount it.
  Both documents' Footnote populations are duplicated (646-b): 21 bodies
  marked out of 53 partly because roughly half of the 53 are second copies
  of the other half. The duplicate prints twice at the page foot, visible in
  the evidence images. Deduplication is upstream and was not attempted here.
  penev_B still emits one `\footnotetext[37]{}` with an empty body — a
  Footnote whose `content` parsed to nothing. It printed empty before too;
  it now prints empty WITH a number.
