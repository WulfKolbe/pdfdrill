407 — the cell route does not hold. 417 proceeds as written.
============================================================

THE QUESTION

The page route builds the report, rasterises each page at 300 and 600 dpi and
compares the two image columns as typeset. The class is not known until the
page exists, which is what forces the two-phase build, the phase stamp and
`--no-legend`.

The cell route compares the same two things one step earlier — a standalone
render of the row's LaTeX against the row's scan crop — so the class would be
known before the first compile and the seven steps could be three.

50 rows of chung2019combinatorics, standalone render at 400 dpi against the
crop, classes compared against report.ink.json.

ANSWER: 36 of 48 agree, 75.0%. It does not hold, for two independent reasons.

MEASURED FAIRLY, AND THE FIRST RUN WAS NOT

My first version passed `scale_stable=False`, so the cell route could not
produce `stable` AT ALL — five rows misclassified by construction, and the
75% would have been 70.8% of my own omission. The page route gets `stable`
from one cell rasterised at 300 and 600 agreeing, so the cell route was given
the same question: render at 400 and 800 and compare. Both numbers are below;
the fair one is the one that decides.

RECALL PER PAGE-ROUTE CLASS

  class          n   kept     %   where the rest went
  noise         34     29    85%   clean 4, stable 1
  component      5      2    40%   noise 2, weak 1
  stable         5      2    40%   noise 2, weak 1
  clean          2      2   100%   —
  weak           1      1   100%   —
  unrendered     1      0     0%   weak 1

REASON 1 — IT LOSES THE CLASS THE REPORT EXISTS FOR

`component` is the actionable class: 9.2% of rows across the published set,
and the thing a reader is scanning for. The cell route keeps 2 of 5. THREE
ROWS STOP BEING RED.

A 75% overall agreement sounds survivable until it is read by class. The
disagreement is not spread evenly over a large `noise` majority — it is
concentrated on the rare rows that matter, which is the worst place for it.
A route that silently drops 60% of the findings is not a faster route to the
same answer; it is a different answer.

`unrendered` it cannot produce at all: demotion is a property of the report,
and the cell route never builds one.

REASON 2 — IT IS NOT EVEN FASTER

  page route   424 s / 411 rows = 1.03 s/row    one rasterised page carries ~4 rows
  cell route   103 s /  48 rows = 2.15 s/row    TWO xelatex compiles per row

Twice as slow. 382's 521 pairs/min was comparison over images ALREADY
rendered — it measured the comparator, not the route, and the render is the
cost. Rasterising one page at 4.6 s and getting four rows beats compiling two
standalone documents per row, and it beats it before any of the accuracy
above is counted.

I quoted that 521 figure myself in 403 as the argument for this route. It was
the right number for the wrong question.

WHAT THIS SETTLES

  - 417 proceeds as written, through the seven-step chain.
  - The two-phase build stays, and 403's conclusion stands for a second
    reason: not only is the colour worth keeping, the fast route that would
    have removed the second compile is slower and less accurate.
  - 400's documentation fix remains the right fix. There was no architecture
    to change.

WHAT IT DOES NOT SETTLE

The two rows that did not compile, and the 4 noise->clean moves, are
scale effects — the in-table cells are 102 mm and 122 mm and a standalone
render is a third scale (340). A cell route that rendered at the TABLE's
width might agree far better. That is a real experiment and it is not this
one; it would still be twice as slow, so it would need a reason other than
speed.

Cost of finding out: about an hour, against a corpus re-measurement of 22
documents that would have had to be redone.
