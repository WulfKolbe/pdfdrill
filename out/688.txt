688 — overprints: one symbol drawn as two glyphs

THE CASE THE USER FOUND
------------------------
`1510.06699_EQ0241` prints a slashed J. The PDF draws it as TWO glyphs at one
place:

    'J'  x 207.84-213.36  top 470.73  CMMI10
    '/'  x 208.56-213.54  top 469.77  CMMI10

MathPix emitted neither, and because the J anchored the numerator it produced
`\frac{...}{}` and `\frac{}{...}` — two fractions each missing a half, joined
by a spurious `=`. The row RENDERS, so no gate saw it. Confidence 0.019.

A NAMED-GLYPH CHECK CANNOT FIND THIS. `missing_glyphs` only sees glyphs the
font had to NAME because Unicode could not map them; a plain "/" maps
perfectly and arrives as a character. Geometry is the only evidence.

THE DETECTOR'S OWN DEFECT, MEASURED AND FIXED
----------------------------------------------
`overprints()` compared START positions within 2.5 pt, because `Glyph` never
stored a width. Two adjacent narrow glyphs are indistinguishable from an
overprint under that test — which is why a division solidus and a prime were
reported. `Glyph.x1` now carries the right edge and the rule is EXTENT
OVERLAP: a mark drawn through a glyph covers most of it, one drawn beside it
touches at an edge. Verified on the real EQ0241 coordinates (kept) and on
`/`-beside-`\sqrt` (dropped).

THREE LICENCE DEFECTS, EACH FOUND BY A ROW IT WRONGLY REFUSED
--------------------------------------------------------------
  * AN OVERPRINT LICENSES A SLASH COMMAND. No single glyph corresponds to the
    macro that writes a slashed symbol; the PAIR does. Without this the route
    refused EQ0241's repair, which was correct and complete.
  * LATEX MARKUP IS NOT A GLYPH. `\cancel{D}` introduces `\cancel`, `{`, `}`.
    Requiring the page to "place" a brace refused cardona EQ0048 outright, and
    counting braces as CONTENT exhausted a three-token budget before anything
    changed (mielke EQ0474: "4 distinct tokens", three of them markup). One row
    was refused for a literal NEWLINE. `STRUCTURE` is now exempt from both
    rules. EQ0241 had passed only by accident — its broken `\frac` left spare
    braces that cancelled out the ones the repair added.
  * A NEGATION SLASH OVER A RELATION IS THE NEGATED RELATION. gilmore EQ0012
    prints a negation slash over `=` in `(23)(12) = (123)`; the mathematics is
    "not equal", and `\cancel{=}` — which the model proposed and which renders
    — says "equals, cancelled". `\neq` is licensed for the relations TeX has a
    negated form for, and nothing wider.

WHAT MADE MINIMAX USEFUL HERE, WHEN IT WAS NOT BEFORE
-------------------------------------------------------
Three earlier attempts got the symbol right and bled content from
neighbouring equations. This time it merged two malformed fractions, dropped
the spurious `=`, and placed the slashed J correctly — because a new third
prompt arm (`refine-propose-census`, 2026-09-14) TOLD it the two glyphs are
one symbol. That fact is unguessable from a crop at these sizes and free from
the PDF.

It also returned `\slashed{J}`, which passes `display_safe` — which does not
check that a command EXISTS — and would then have failed at xelatex, because
`slashed` is not in the preamble. `cancel` is (report_tex.py:811), so the
route licenses only `\cancel` and `\not`: a token it authorises must be one
the build can typeset. My earlier claim that this work needed a preamble
change was wrong; nothing about the preamble moved.
