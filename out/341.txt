341 - bothways: is the crop a partial view, or is the pairing wrong?
====================================================================

NEITHER. The pairing is CORRECT and the crop is the WHOLE composed
figure. The dimensions disagree because the composition is not the base.

THE TWO IMAGES

  base   figures/ch1/bothways.jpg    725 x 538 px, 132 dpi, aspect 1.348
         two curved arrows forming a cycle, and nothing else.

  crop   ...-038_197_740_257_421.jpg 740 x 197 px, aspect 3.756
         the same two arrows PLUS the words "density operator" on the
         left and "probability distribution" on the right, set between
         them.

  The crop file's own dimensions match its 5-tuple exactly (740 x 197),
  so the filename is not lying about the rectangle.

THE SOURCE SAYS WHY

  chapters/two_tufte.tex:686

    \begin{tikzpicture}
    \node at (0,0)   {\includegraphics[scale=0.1]{figures/ch1/bothways}};
    \node at (-2,0)  {density operator};
    \node at (2.2,0) {probability distribution};
    \end{tikzpicture}

  No trim, no clip. The image is placed at the origin and TWO TEXT NODES
  are placed either side of it on the same baseline. The composition is
  therefore much wider than the image and no taller, which is exactly
  the shape the crop has. Every mark in the crop is accounted for: the
  arrows are the base, the words are the two sibling nodes.

IS THERE A BETTER CANDIDATE ON THAT PAGE?

  No. Page 38 carries exactly ONE crop, the one paired. There is nothing
  else it could be.

WHAT THIS SETTLES, AND WHAT IT DOES NOT

  It settles that an aspect-ratio disagreement between base and crop is
  NOT evidence of a wrong pairing when the inclusion is annotated. 301
  measured four tikz-composed pairs differing by up to 2.4x and read
  that as "expected"; this one differs by 2.8x and is right. Shape can
  corroborate a pairing between a plain figure and its crop. For an
  annotated one it cannot, in either direction.

  It also shows the labels sit BETWEEN the arrows -- inside the base's
  own rectangle, not outside it. A prompt or detector that assumes
  annotation surrounds the figure would place these two wrong. That is
  342's question and this is one instance of it.

METHOD NOTE

  I first predicted the composed aspect from the node coordinates and
  got 7.34 against a measured 3.76, and the gap was entirely my own
  invented text widths -- I had guessed "about 2.6 cm and 4.2 cm". The
  number was discarded rather than reported: a prediction built on made
  up inputs is not a measurement, and looking at the two images settled
  in one step what the arithmetic could not.
