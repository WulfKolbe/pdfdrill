113a - failure counts, both scorings, and B split by the S notation
==============================================================================

PER VARIANT, BOTH SCORINGS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   variant   n  failed  compiled   median over    median over ALL
                                    compiled      (failures = baseline)
   A        19       5        14          +146                 +4
   B        19       1        18          +196               +185
   C        19       4        15            -8                 -4
   D        19       4        15            -6                 -2

THE TWO MEDIANS DISAGREE BY MORE THAN THE VARIANTS DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   A moves from +146 to +4 depending only on how a failure is scored.
   That is a larger swing than the gap between the best and worst variant
   under either scoring, so the choice of scoring rule is not a detail -
   it is the headline.

   Neither number is the honest one on its own:
     over COMPILED replies    - measures quality WHERE the model produced
       usable LaTeX, and silently rewards a variant that fails often, since
       its failures are simply absent from the denominator. A prompt that
       compiled once and improved that row would score best of all.
     failures scored AT BASELINE - assumes a failed reply costs nothing,
       i.e. that we fall back to MathPix's reading. That is true of this
       pipeline and it flatters the failure-prone variants by treating a
       non-answer as a neutral one.

   Read together: A and B are bad under both. C and D improve under both,
   and their advantage SHRINKS under the second scoring (-8 -> -4, -6 ->
   -2) because their gains are diluted by four failures each.

B SPLIT BY WHETHER THE CROP CONTAINS A BOLD OR SCRIPT S
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   contains S    n= 1  compiled= 1  median(compiled)=    +0  median(all)=    +0
   no S          n=18  compiled=17  median(compiled)=  +207  median(all)=  +196

   THE SPLIT IS 1 AGAINST 18 AND THAT IS THE FINDING.

   Exactly one of the 19 trial crops contains an S in the sense B's hint
   addresses. A median over one row is that row, not a median, and no
   comparison between the two groups is supportable.

   So B's +196 is the cost of the hint on crops the hint does not apply
   to. It was never measured on the population it was written for, and
   this trial cannot measure it: out/104 found the discrete-integral S in
   2 expressions across four Heim books, and this set is drawn from the
   arXiv corpus by confidence, which selects for something else entirely.

   Testing B properly needs a trial set built FROM the notation - the
   BH1org_OCR page 117 rows and whatever else a bold-S scan turns up -
   not from a confidence threshold. That is a different set and a
   different task.

A CAVEAT ON THE DETECTOR
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   'contains an S' was decided from MathPix's OWN latex for the row, which
   is circular in exactly the cases that matter: if MathPix misread the S
   as \sum or as something else, the detector does not see it and the crop
   lands in the 'no S' group. 3 of the 19 contain \sum, which is the
   substitution B exists to prevent.

   The non-circular detector is the scan image, and reading it is what the
   model under test is for. So the split is reported as measured and its
   direction of error is stated: it can only UNDERCOUNT the S group.
