126 — THE CONFLICT, BECAUSE IT DECIDES WHAT GETS BUILT
-------------------------------------------------------
Measured in 0902.0431 just now:

   values whose \begin{X}/\end{X} counts differ : 24
   unmatched environment                       : itemize, all 24
   of those now REPAIRED and rendering         : 24  (all of them)

Those 24 are the same rows as the \end{itemize} fragments the task points at.
They were fixed on 23 Aug (commit 518c548): MathPix glues a stray closer onto
display math that ends a list —

   \[ (A \vee B)^{\prime}=-B \vee A . \] \end{itemize}

— and renderable() now drops a TRAILING \end{X} that has no matching \begin{X}.
That took 0902.0431 from 31 unrendered equations to 7, at confidences up to
1.000.

126 as written says "reject any value whose counts differ". Implemented
literally it re-blanks all 24 and undoes that recovery.

The two readings are both defensible and they are not the same task:

   (a) REJECT — treat the fragment as a broken object and refuse to render it.
       0902.0431 goes back to ~31 unrendered. Correct if the position is that a
       value carrying prose structure is malformed and must not be presented as
       mathematics.

   (b) REPORT — keep the repair, and add the count + environment as a REPORTED
       defect on the object (a flag in the model, a line in the QC output),
       so the 24 broken objects are visible and countable without blanking
       equations that render correctly once the stray closer is dropped.

I read the task's own words as (b) — "a defect producing broken objects, and
cheap to detect" is about DETECTION — but the verb it uses is "reject", which
is (a). I am not guessing between them, because the difference is 24 equations
in one document and the same class corpus-wide.

Say (a) or (b) and 126 is short work either way. 125 needs nothing from you;
127 needs the four names.
