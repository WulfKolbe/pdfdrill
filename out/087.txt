087 - body signatures: do listings have an identity without captions?
==============================================================================

THE ANSWER IS NO, AND IN TWO SEPARATE WAYS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   listings                                    426
     with a signature at all                   105  (25%)
     signature EMPTY - define nothing top-level 321  (75%)

   Among the 105 that have one, within their own document:
     unique       48  (46%)
     colliding    57  (54%)   in 12 groups

   So 48 of 426 listings — 11% — have a unique content-derived identity
   within their document. The interface idea does not survive this as
   stated, and needs refining before anything depends on it.

FAILURE 1 - 75% DEFINE NOTHING AT THE TOP LEVEL
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   The larger failure by far, and it is not a parser gap. Most listings in
   papers are FRAGMENTS: a shell transcript, a config blob, three lines of
   a loop body, a JSON payload, a prompt. There is no definition in them to
   name because the author was showing an excerpt, not a unit of code.

   Concentrated in the papers with the most listings:
     2009.11564                      36
     arXiv_2009.11564                36
     2404.04837v3                    33
     2407.11418v1                    28
     2604.22294                      25
     1804.10694v5                    23

   An empty signature must NEVER be used as a key. Two listings that define
   nothing are not the same listing, and treating them as equal is the
   UNKNOWN==UNKNOWN defect (HANDOVER-RULES rule 5) in a new costume — the
   one that cost this project a retracted noise floor. A test pins it.

FAILURE 2 - THE COLLISIONS ARE MEANINGFUL, WHICH IS WORSE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   54% of the signatures that exist collide within their own document, and
   the collisions are not accidents:

     2310.02304v3                 x15  ['improve_algorithm']
     2307.13528v2                 x6   ['truncate_number']
     2307.13528v2                 x6   ['monotonic']
     2307.13528v2                 x6   ['fizz_buzz']
     2408.08435v1                 x5   ['forward']
     2310.02304v3                 x4   ['utility']
     2310.02304v3                 x4   ['algorithm']
     2307.07924v4                 x3   ['GUI']

   2310.02304v3 defines improve_algorithm 15 times. 2307.13528v2 defines
   truncate_number, monotonic and fizz_buzz 6 times each. These are papers
   that show SUCCESSIVE VERSIONS of the same function — the exact case the
   git-revision question is about.

   So the signature does the opposite of what was hoped: where revisions
   exist it identifies the FUNCTION and cannot distinguish the REVISIONS,
   which is precisely where distinguishing them matters. A key that
   collides hardest on the case it was meant to serve is not a key.

WHAT THIS LEAVES
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   out/084 found 227 listings with neither language nor caption.
   22 of them have a signature. The other 205 have no identity by
   any route this session has built: no type, no name, no label, no
   derivable content key.

   What every listing does have is position — source_file plus source_line,
   set on every one of the 426 objects. It is stable within a revision of
   the paper and changes when the paper is edited, which is the correct
   behaviour for a location and the wrong behaviour for an identity.

   If a stable identity is needed the honest candidates are: a hash of the
   body (distinguishes revisions, but any whitespace edit is a new object),
   or signature PLUS ordinal within the document (improve_algorithm #7),
   which is what a reader of 2310.02304v3 actually uses. Neither is built
   here and neither should be chosen from this file alone — the point of
   087 was to test the proposed key, and it fails.
