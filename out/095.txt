I DID NOT IMPLEMENT THE RULE AS WRITTEN, AND THIS IS WHY
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   The task said trailing runs only. Implemented literally, that regressed the
   case the feature was built for. tests/test_tailsplit.py carries the user's
   own motivating example from P7:

     \mathrm{n a c h\;A d d i t i o n}\; S...=\phi(n_2)-\phi(n_1-1),
     \;\mathrm{w a s~i m~V e r-}

   "nach Addition" LEADS and is genuine commentary: strip it and a complete
   equation remains. Two tests failed immediately, and they were right to.

   So the rule is not "leading runs are never commentary" — it is that a
   leading run may be the SUBJECT, and the difference is what is left behind:

     \mathrm{nach Addition}\; S...=\phi(...)   -> remainder is an equation
     \text{Pack}_{<\omega} \text{-complete}     -> remainder is _{<\omega}

   A remainder that opens with _ or ^ has lost the thing it modified. That is
   the test, and it separates the two cases exactly: 3 of the 7 leading
   removals stop, including out/093's row, and 4 continue.

THE TRAILING RULE, AS ASKED
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   Strip a trailing run only where mathematics precedes the prose — tested on
   the line's OPENING token, applied to what remains once any leading run has
   been taken. That last clause matters: the founding example opens with prose
   and its tail still legitimately follows mathematics.

     x^{2}+y^{2}=z^{2} \text{ where n is even }   splits
     \text{Pack}_{<\omega} \text{-complete }      does not
     \mathrm{d}x \text{ with respect to time }    does not

WHY THE OPENING TOKEN AND NOT THE RESIDUE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   The first attempt tested what SURVIVES when the \text{} groups are removed.
   It accepted the 093 row and rejected the legitimate one, because
   `\text{pack}_{2}` leaves "2" and `_{<\omega}` leaves "<". A lone subscript
   fragment reads as mathematics and is not.

   The opening token is cruder and correct, and it fails safe: when wrong it
   declines to split, and a value left whole loses only a separation.

   5 tests added; 2,142 pass, including the two that caught the over-broad
   first rule.
