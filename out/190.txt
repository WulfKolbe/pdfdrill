190 — the five real defects: the list for MathPix
==============================================================================
Five equations MathPix read wrongly, surviving every filter in out/187–189.
All five are at high confidence — three at 1.000 — which is the point: MathPix
reports no doubt about any of them.

Each entry carries the bibkey, the identifier, the page, the confidence, the
CDN crop of the scan MathPix itself produced, the LaTeX it emitted, and what
the equation should read. The crop URL is included so the source image can be
looked at directly rather than taken on my description.

Two classes, and they are not equally serious. The two CJK ideographs are
VISIBLE — any reader sees a Chinese character in an equation. The other four
are INVISIBLE: they render as clean, plausible mathematics that is wrong, and
nothing in the output says so. An `l` in an incidence matrix and a matrix with
a missing entry both typeset perfectly.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

each of the 6 was classified by hand (out/189): 5 real, 1 a false positive of
my own check. The sixth entry below was found separately, by verifying all 918
rows the fix had CLEARED against each array's own declared column count.

Confidence is MathPix's own value, taken from lines.json unmodified.

WHAT IS NOT ON THIS LIST, AND WHY
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
One survivor was excluded after inspection:

   Lie Groups, Physics and Geometry (Robert Gilmore)_EQ0443   p135  conf 1.000
   e^{r a^†} e^{δI} e^{l a} = [1 0 0; 0 1 r][1 0 δ; 0 1 0][1 l 0; 0 1 0; 0 0 1]

My confusable-cell check flagged the `l`. It is wrong: r, δ and l are the
three parameters of the group element, and they appear as matrix entries
because that is exactly what the identity says. MathPix read it correctly.
That is a defect in my check, not in their output, and sending it would waste
their time and cost this list its credibility.

SCOPE — what this list is and is not
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
It is every defect that survives the structural checks at confidence >= 0.9.
It is NOT every defect MathPix made. The checks look for: unbalanced
environments, non-uniform table widths, CJK characters, and a lone `l` in a
numeric table. A wrong exponent, a transposed pair of digits, or a misread
Greek letter passes all four and is invisible here.

out/189 measured the reach of the narrowest of those checks: the letter-for-
digit class has ONE member corpus-wide that this apparatus can see. Whether it
has more that the apparatus cannot see is not established, and I would not
claim otherwise.
