WHAT INKDRILL ACTUALLY SEES

Nine separate runs in the 18-page report, broken by column width:

    ord 1   6 cols   page 1              <- selected, holds 3 rows
    ord 2   3 cols   page 2
    ord 3   5 cols   page 3
    ord 4   3 cols   page 4
    ord 5   6 cols   pages 5-6
    ord 6   5 cols   pages 7-8
    ord 7   6 cols   page 9
    ord 8   3 cols   page 10
    ord 9   5 cols   pages 11-18

`inkmeasure.measure` asks for table=1, checks only that its column count
matches (6 == 6), and hands it to `_pages_for`, which then walks pages
looking for 60 rows and finds 3.

The Display-equations rows are spread across ordinals 1, 5 and 7 - every
6-column run - and the Inline-formulas rows across 3, 6 and 9. The full
listing interleaves two tables of different widths, and inkdrill breaks a run
wherever the visual column structure changes. The manifest counts one logical
longtable; inkdrill counts one detected run; in the findings shape those were
the same object because the tables were short and contiguous, and in the full
listing they are not.

So this is the reason ten of twenty-one documents STOPPED at step 3 in Stage
C, and unbounding makes it worse rather than better: more pages, more breaks,
more fragments.

WHY I AM NOT GIVING A NUMBER

I could multiply 8.4 s of segmentation and a per-page rasterise cost by 21
and produce a figure. It would be a projection of a run that cannot happen,
and the thing it would be projecting - the row-run join - is exactly what is
broken. A number computed from a measurement that never completed is the
defect this project has spent four tasks removing.

What is honest: the BUILD is cheap (2.9 s for 18 pages; the largest documents
were 20-66 s in Stage C). The measurement cost is unknown because no document
has ever measured a full listing.

WHAT WOULD MAKE IT ANSWERABLE

`_pages_for` and the ordinal-1 assumption need to accept a table that arrives
as several runs: select every run whose column count matches and whose pages
are consistent with the manifest, rather than the first one. That is a change
to inkmeasure, not to the report, and until it exists the full listing cannot
be measured at any price.

The alternative you named - measure the full listing on demand rather than in
the pipeline - has the same blocker. It is not a scheduling question.
