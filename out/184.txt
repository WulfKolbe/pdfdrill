FIRST CAVEAT — COMPILE AND INK ARE MUTUALLY EXCLUSIVE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The question asks for equations that fail a structural check AND have an ink
baseline. For the compile check those two conditions cannot both hold.

An ink baseline is the distance between a RENDER of MathPix's reading and the
scan crop. If the reading does not compile there is no render, so there is no
baseline. Measured: 52 of the 110 sampled structural failures (47%) do not
compile, and every one of them is unmeasurable for that reason.

So "fails compile AND ink above the floor" is an empty set, not a small one,
and the ~2,148 compile-only failures estimated above are reachable by neither
selector — not by confidence, which reports no doubt, and not by ink, which
has nothing to measure. They are a third population and they need a third
detector, which is just "try to compile it": cheap, exhaustive, and not
currently run anywhere in the pipeline.

SECOND CAVEAT — THE NOISE FLOOR IS NOT 7, AND THIS IS THE REAL FINDING
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
I measured the baseline distribution of the CLEAN population before using any
threshold, and it is nothing like the row-metric's floor:

   conf >= 0.9, passes every structural check, n=100 measured
      ink d   min 0   p50 39   p75 75   p90 141   p95 166   max 638
      above 7: 87 of 100

Eighty-seven percent of structurally clean, high-confidence equations sit
above inkdrill's noise floor of 7. That floor belongs to inkdrill's ROW-level
five-tuple measured from a finished report page; applying it to my crop ink
distance would flag seven equations in eight as suspect, and out/119 already
established what those flags are: 18 of 20 examined by eye were purely
typographic, 0 of 20 carried a symbol MathPix got wrong.

So the honest floor for this metric is empirical, and I have taken it from the
clean population itself rather than borrowing one: p90 = 141, the level
exceeded by one clean equation in ten. Against that floor:

   structural failures above 141   19 of 56 measured   33.9%
   clean rows above 141            10 of 100           10.0%   (by construction)

The separation is real — 3.4x — but it is a separation between distributions,
not a clean cut. At any threshold that catches most defects, most of what it
catches is typography.

EQ0526 IS NOT IN THIS POPULATION, AND MY OWN OUT/178 IS WHY IT LOOKED LIKE IT
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The task names EQ0526 as an example. It is not one, and the error is mine.

out/178 said EQ0526 "carries MathPix confidence 1.0 and is genuinely defective
— nine cells in an eight-column array". The nine-cells defect belongs to
GROK'S PROPOSAL for EQ0526, which that task rejected. MathPix's own reading of
EQ0526 passes all four structural checks, including a clean standalone xelatex
compile — verified directly. I read a defect off a proposal and recorded it as
a property of the equation. out/178 is corrected in place.

What is true of EQ0526 is a sharper case than the one I claimed:

   MathPix confidence   1.0
   structural checks    ALL PASS
   crop ink baseline    38   (scan 21 components / 6 holes,
                              render 56 components / 9 holes)
   its scan crop carries ink across x 264..795 of 1244 px; the render spans
   16..1233 — three times the ink density of the region it claims to transcribe

So EQ0526 belongs to a FOURTH population: high confidence, structurally clean,
and in substantial disagreement with its own scan. That is harder for
confidence selection than a malformed equation, not easier — there is nothing
to find by reading the LaTeX at all. But at a baseline of 38 it sits below the
clean p50 of 39, so an ink selector using the measured floor would not reach
it either.

That is worth stating plainly rather than dressing up: on the evidence I have,
neither selector reaches EQ0526, and I do not have a threshold that separates
it from ordinary typographic disagreement.

WHAT THIS SAYS ABOUT ink-SELECTED REFINE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The claim in out/178 was that an ink-selected pass reaches a population a
confidence-selected one cannot. That survives, but smaller and differently
shaped than it looked:

   reachable by ink selection and not by confidence   ~318   (est.)
   reachable by NEITHER, needing only a compile     ~2,148   (est.)

The larger and cheaper win is not ink selection at all. It is that ~2,148
equations at confidence >= 0.9 do not compile, nothing in the pipeline
currently tries to compile them, and finding them costs one xelatex run each
with no API calls and no rendering comparison.

I am not building that under this task. 184 asked for a count and this is the
count; proposing a selector is a different decision with a different cost, and
widening the scope is how a report stops answering the question it was given.

METHOD, SO THE NUMBERS CAN BE CHECKED
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   stage 1  every model, every Equation with confidence >= 0.9, checked against
            changereq.check_uniform_widths, env_balance.env_defect and
            report_tex.cjk_defect. Exhaustive: 1,345 documents.
   stage 2  two samples of 110, seed 184, each measured end to end — scan crop
            at the page's own MathPix width, standalone xelatex render, two
            inkdrill runs, crop ink distance. render_latex IS the compile, so
            the compile rate falls out of the same pass.

   Skips are counted, not dropped: 7 of A and 2 of B could not be measured
   (6 blank references, 2 no crop, 1 other) and are excluded from the ink
   percentages rather than treated as zeros.
