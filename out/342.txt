====================================================================

  annotated inclusions classified : 221

  OVERLAPPING  a sibling falls INSIDE the image's rectangle : 114
  AROUND       every sibling is outside it                  : 79

  unparsed     non-numeric coordinates, e.g. (a.north)      : 11

  Of the 193 with a verdict, 59% overlap. A prompt that says 'the text
  is above the figure' or a detector that looks only around the border is
  wrong for the majority, not for a fringe.

HOW THE EXTENT IS COMPUTED, AND HOW IT WAS CALIBRATED

  The image's rectangle is its natural size scaled by the `scale=` or
  `width=` option, centred on its node coordinate. Two things had to be
  measured rather than assumed, and both were wrong first:

  1. The image's own \node counted itself as a sibling at distance zero,
     so EVERY row came back overlapping -- 210 of 210. A result with no
     second category is not a finding, it is a broken test.

  2. Natural size is 1 bp per pixel -- 72 dpi -- NOT the dpi in the file.
     bothways is a 725x538 JPEG carrying 132 dpi metadata, included at
     scale=0.1. pdfplumber measures it at 72.5 x 53.8 pt on page 38 of
     the author's own PDF. At 72 dpi the formula gives 72.6 x 53.9; at
     the file's 132 it gives 39.7 x 29.5, an extent 1.83x too small that
     biases every verdict toward 'around'. Fixing it moved the split from
     104/89 to 114/79.

  So the classifier is calibrated against a rectangle measured from the
  compiled PDF, not against its own arithmetic.

WHAT 337 SHOULD SAY

  For an AROUND row the description can name a direction -- the sibling
  coordinates give left/right/above/below relative to the image's centre,
  and its extent gives the distance.

  For an OVERLAPPING row it should say overlapping and not invent a
  direction. 'Centred above at distance d' is false for a label sitting
  on the figure, and a model told something false about the picture it
  can see is worse off than one told nothing.
