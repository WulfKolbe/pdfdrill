POPULATION - WHY 42 DOCUMENTS AND NOT 49
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   The corpus is 49 documents. An SLT distance needs the AUTHOR'S LaTeX,
   so `injectlatex` was run over all of them first, and:
      48  resolved to a genuine author e-print (arxiv .tgz)
       1  1012.3259 resolved to the MathPix .tex.zip instead - MathPix's own
          reconstruction, so its 'author' side would BE MathPix and every
          row would be a manufactured SLT-zero. EXCLUDED, as in out/040.
      42  of the 48 actually contribute rows; the other 6 matched no
          equations at the 0.55 similarity threshold, so they have no
          SLT-zero rows to emit rather than being dropped for cause.

WHAT THE ROWS SAY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   Content is identical by construction on every row, so each ink distance
   is the metric's own noise for render-vs-scan, never a disagreement.

      min 0   p50 2   p75 6   p90 14   p95 22   p99 43   max 243   mean 5.7
      above  6 :  364  (24.5%)

   p95 = 22, UNCHANGED from the 808-row sample in out/051.txt even though

================================================================================
CORRECTION (2026-08-21) - THE FLOOR OF 22 IS RETRACTED; IT IS 7
================================================================================

   The 'content is identical by construction' claim above was FALSE for
   nearly half these rows. mathgold's parse_latex_slt collapses a
   \begin{aligned} block to one UNRESOLVED node, so two unrelated multi-line
   equations get byte-identical signatures and the SLT distance returns 0
   without comparing contents. Those rows entered this file as 'SLT-zero'
   while their contents genuinely differ.

   The file now carries a DEGENERATE column so the split can be applied by
   whoever reads it rather than trusted from here:

      genuine     n=  826   p50 1   p95 7   max 39
      degenerate  n=  662   p50 6   p95 29   max 243

   The render-vs-scan floor on rows whose content ACTUALLY agrees is
   p95 = 7, not 22. The consumer's original floor of 6 was right to
   within one unit; my '24.5% false positives' was my own metric's defect
   measured back at me. Re-flagging on 22 would have dropped roughly 640
   real findings out of their channel - hiding defects, which is worse than
   the false positives I was warning about.

   Verified from the other side by an independent route (testing each row's
   latex for a multi-line environment rather than using my signatures):
   45.4% degenerate against my 44.5%, genuine p95 7 both ways.

   WHY EVERY CHECK PASSED ANYWAY, which is the part worth keeping: the 22
   survived a doubled sample, held across 42 documents, and agreed within
   one unit with an independently written render route. All true, and none
   of it helped - every check was downstream of the same broken selection
   rule. A control group is only as good as the rule that built it, and
   'content is identical' was an assumption inherited from the metric
   rather than a property anyone verified.
