183 — the reader-facing description for reports/index.html
==============================================================================
WRITTEN, AND WHERE IT IS
   out/183/index-description.html — a drop-in <section> for the top of
   pdfdrill.github.io/reports/index.html, above the document table.

It is not installed. reports/index.html belongs to the pages session, which
now runs a deploy gate asserting that the index and the artefacts agree;
editing it from here would either trip that gate or silently contradict their
table. The markup has been sent to them to land.

WHAT IT SAYS, IN ONE PARAGRAPH
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   Each report pairs MathPix's reading of an equation with the scan it was
   read from. inkdrill typesets that reading, renders it, and compares the
   render against the scan crop by CONNECTED COMPONENTS — the separate marks
   of ink each image contains — and reports the difference between them.

   C|+4 reads: component class, four more marks in the scan than in the
   render. The letter is the class of difference; the signed number is how
   many marks the scan has that the render does not.

   K clean · N noise · W weak · S stable · C component

THE CLAIM THE PAGE HAS TO MAKE, AND THE EVIDENCE UNDER IT
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   A flag on an equation MathPix is confident about is usually a RENDERING
   difference, not a reading error.

That is out/119: twenty flagged rows at high confidence, examined by eye one
at a time.

   typographic                    18
   typographic + region overrun    1
   borderline content              1
   unambiguous content             0

   Not one of the twenty carried a symbol MathPix had got wrong.

The description names what they actually disagreed about, because "18 of 20
were typographic" is a number a reader has to take on trust while the list is
something they can recognise on their own page:

   delimiter auto-sizing   13   \left( \right) vs fixed-size parentheses
   trailing punctuation     8   lives in a separate field, omitted by design
   accent width             3   \bar vs the page's wider \overline
   fraction style           2   \frac vs \dfrac / \tfrac
   upright vs italic Phi    2
   limit placement          1   \int_a^b beside the sign vs \int\limits

WHY THE PAGE SAYS "A PLACE TO LOOK, NOT A VERDICT"
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A residual column on a public page invites exactly one misreading — that a
flagged row is a wrong row — and 18 of 20 says that misreading is wrong most
of the time. The description therefore states what the number IS (two images
differ, by this much) rather than what a reader would assume it implies, and
points at where it earns its keep: a row that is BOTH doubted by MathPix and
in disagreement with its own scan. A confident row with a flag is, on this
evidence, far more likely to be typesetting.

That is also the honest reading of the C|+4 sign convention: more marks in the
scan than the render is what trailing punctuation and wider accents look like.
The commonest cause of a positive component delta in the measured set is not a
missing symbol, it is a symbol set differently.

IT ALSO COVERS THE ABSENT CASE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The final paragraph matches the on-page note added in 180, in the same two
states, so the index and the report cannot disagree with each other:

   "either no measurement has been run, or the report's own table could not be
    read reliably enough to pair measurements with equations. The confidence
    column is unaffected and still applies."

NO INTERNAL VOCABULARY
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The description says "connected components — the separate marks of ink each
image contains" and never says lattice, enclosure, five-tuple, row coverage or
median-fill. A reader does not need any of that to understand what was
compared with what.
