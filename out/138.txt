"MAY HAVE PRODUCED WRONG EXPANSIONS" — HOW MANY ACTUALLY DID
-------------------------------------------------------------
A redefinition is not by itself a wrong expansion. Many are the same body
written twice (a macro defined identically in two included files), where
first-wins and last-wins agree.

Both macro tables were built for all 62 documents — the current code and a
replica of the pre-137 behaviour — and compared body by body:

   documents with a redefinition          62
   ... where the two tables AGREE         29     identical bodies, harmless
   ... where the EXPANSION DIFFERS        33     wrong under first-wins
   macro names affected in total          51

So the exposed population is 33 documents, not 62. Reporting 62 would have
overstated it by 88%.

WHY ONLY ONE OF THE 33 WAS EVER VISIBLE
----------------------------------------
preprints202505.0818.v1 was found because \begin{slide} does not typeset — it
produced an unbalanced-environment flag that out/126's balance check caught.

The other 32 expanded to something that renders. `rvu` recurs across five
documents; `\r` and `\etal` in 2303.03192; `\ba`, `\bb`, `\bz`, `\O` in
2006.14038. A wrong body that still typesets leaves no error, no warning and no
flag — it just means the stored LaTeX is not what the author wrote. Those are
silently wrong and were never going to surface on their own.

That is the argument for fixing the ORDER rather than detecting the symptom:
the balance check found one document out of 33, and only because that
particular wrong expansion happened to be malformed.
