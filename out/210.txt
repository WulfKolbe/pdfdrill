So 614 is a population to look at, not a defect count. Reporting it as
"614 arity errors" would repeat the mistake out/197 found in the acronym rule
and out/187 found in my own confusable set: a detector whose first version
invents a false-positive class to replace the one it removes.

THE MIDDLE TIER IS WHERE THE REAL YIELD IS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Restricting to the turnstile family — the relations that provably cannot take
indices, since ⊢ and ⊨ join two expressions and are not operators:

    11  2010.14265                          X \nvdash_{P} \{Y, Z\}
     2  Seven Sketches (Fong & Spivak)
     2  An Invitation to Applied Category Theory
     2  OMDoc markup format
     1  0707.4470
     1  Geometric, Algebraic and Topological Methods for QFT
     1  Geometrodynamics (Mielke)          <- EQ0873

2010.14265's eleven are one defect repeated:

   X \nvdash_{P} \{Y, Z\}
   \forall S \subseteq V \setminus \{X,Y\}: X \nVdash_{P} Y \mid S

That is CONDITIONAL INDEPENDENCE — X ⫫_P Y | S, the double-perpendicular of
probabilistic graphical models. MathPix reached for \nvdash to draw ⫫, which
is the same failure as EQ0873 in a different notation: a glyph produced by a
command whose meaning is unrelated to it. out/208 inferred this from context
and could not verify it, because the objects are inline formulas with no CDN
crop. The arity signature reaches them without needing one.

WHY THIS DETECTOR MATTERS: IT NEEDS NOTHING BUT THE STRING
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
EQ0873 passes every check in this sequence. It compiles. Its environments
balance. Its table widths are uniform. It has no CJK and no confusable cell.
It sits at confidence 0.99951171875 — MathPix's near-maximum. out/208 found it
only because out/207 asked why a turnstile had indices, and confirming it took
a human looking at a crop.

This check needs no crop, no render, no model, no API call. `\nvdash^{A}{ }_{B}`
is ill-formed as a string, whatever it renders as, because a binary relation
is not a tensor. It is the first detector in this sequence that could have
found EQ0873 unaided.

AND THE HONEST LIMIT OF THAT CLAIM
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The sharp detector was DESIGNED from EQ0873. Finding EQ0873 is therefore not
independent evidence that it works — it is the fit, not a test of the fit. Its
precision on this corpus is 1 of 1, and that number means less than it looks.

What can be said without overclaiming:

   * it costs one regex over the stored LaTeX, and it is exhaustive;
   * on the corpus it produces ONE finding and no false positives, so it is
     not a queue anybody has to triage;
   * its recall is unknown and probably poor. It catches the tensor-index
     shape ^{X}{ }_{Y} only. A relation with a single index — the eleven
     conditional-independence cases — needs the middle tier, which does NOT
     have clean precision and needs the relation restricted to the turnstile
     family by hand.

The generalisation that survives is narrower than "arity mismatch" and worth
stating in its own words: A SYMBOL'S LATEX NAME CARRIES AN ARITY, AND THE
SURROUNDING SYNTAX CARRIES ANOTHER. Where they disagree, the name is usually
wrong — because OCR picks a command by how the glyph LOOKS, and a glyph's
appearance says nothing about how many arguments its command takes.
